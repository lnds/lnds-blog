 It seems we never learn, and people keep insisting on trying to
control the internet, as if it were a thing that can be owned.

Maybe it's worth remembering what
[Searls](http://blogs.law.harvard.edu/doc/) and
[Weinberger](http://www.evident.com/) told us a few years ago about the
Internet:

The Internet cannot be owned, not even by the companies through whose
"pipes" it flows, because it is *an agreement, not a thing. The Internet
is not only in the public domain, it **is** a public domain.*

Five years ago Searls and Weinberger left us [World of
Ends](http://www.worldofends.com/), which we can read in our own
language thanks to the translation work of Javier Smaldone. If you have
never read this text, I invite you to get to know it, or to come back to
it, so we don't forget the fundamentals about the internet:

# **World** of **Ends** 

## What the Internet is and how to stop mistaking it for something else 

**by Doc Searls and David Weinberger**

The text opens by separating the mistakes we learn from, like thinking
that selling pet toys on the Web is a great way to get rich, from the
ones we insist on making over and over: treating the Web like
television, as something carriers must filter, control and "improve", or
as something lacking regulation to protect the industries that feel
threatened by it. The authors call it Repetitive Mistake Syndrome, recall
[John Gilmore](http://www.toad.com/gnu/)'s famous line that the Internet
interprets censorship as damage and routes around it, and reduce the
cure to one simple fact: the Net is **a world of ends**. You are at one
end; everything and everyone else are at the other ends.

![](1008231_network_spheres.jpg)

From there it lays out a handful of statements. These are their titles,
with the gist of each; the complete text is at the link at the end.

## 1. The Internet isn't complicated

It was designed to be the simplest conceivable way to get bits from any
A to any B.

## 2. The Internet isn't a thing. It's an agreement

It isn't wiring, a system, or a source of programming. It is just a
protocol, an agreement about how things work together.

## 3. The Internet is stupid

On purpose[^1]. It knows only one thing: this bunch of bits needs to get
from one end of the Net to another. Stupidity is robust.

## 4. Adding value to the Internet lowers its value 

Optimize a network for one kind of application and you make it worse
for the others.

## 5. All the Internet's value grows on its edges 

Search, like everything else, is a service built at one of the millions
of ends, with nobody's permission. The Internet was created as a free
market for innovation.

## 6. Money moves to the suburbs

Connectivity becomes a commodity, and it should be allowed to; every
attempt to add value to the Internet itself must be
resisted[^2].

## 7. The end of the world? Nah, the world of ends 

Craig Burton describes the Net's stupid architecture as a hollow sphere
made up entirely of ends[^3]. Because the Internet is an agreement, it
belongs to no person or group: not the backbone companies, not the
ISPs, not any government.

![](1024879_-world_background_iv-.jpg)

## 8. The Internet's three virtues 

Three basic rules of behavior tied directly to the nature of the
Internet.

### 8.a. No one owns it {#8anadielaposee}

This is the passage quoted at the top of this post.

# 8.b. Everyone can use it {#8btodospuedenusarla}

The Net puts no obstacle in the way of your participation; permissions
are deliberately left outside the system.

### 8.c. Anyone can improve it {#8ccualquierapuedemejorarla}

Build a service at the edge, or better yet enable new ones through a
new agreement, as long as it is open, non-proprietary and for everyone.

## 9. If the Internet is so simple, why are so many confused about it? 

Because its three virtues are the antithesis of how governments and
businesses see the world.

## 10. Some mistakes we can stop making already 

A closing list aimed at the record industry, government officials,
telecom carriers, spectrum regulators, would-be censors and advertisers.
It ends by saying we have nothing to lose but our stupidity.
