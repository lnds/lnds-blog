> "If you look at software today, through the lens of the history of engineering, it's certainly engineering of a sort—but it's the kind of engineering that people without the concept of the arch did.

> Most software today is very much like an [Egyptian pyramid](/en/blog/lnds/2008/01/22/the-mystery-of-the-pyramids/) with millions of bricks piled on top of each other, with no structural integrity, but just done by brute force and thousands of slaves."

Alan Kay in [an interview for Queue magazine](http://acmqueue.com/modules.php?name=Content&pa=printer_friendly&pid=273&page=1), to which the interviewer replies

> The analogy is even better because there are the hidden chambers that nobody can understand.
