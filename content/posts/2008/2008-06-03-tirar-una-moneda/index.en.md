The model behind the air quality forecasting system has an [error rate of 50%](http://www.radio.uchile.cl/notas.aspx?idNota=45510).

In the words of our [dear minister Vidal](/en/blog/lnds/2008/05/11/the-ministers-digital-illiteracy/):

> "The predictive model has to be fixed; if it fails, it has to be fixed," and he added, "let's be frank, a mistake was made here, and the reason for the mistake is a forecasting system with far too wide a margin, on the order of 50 percent. What Intendente Erazo has said is that we need a predictive model that is much more predictive and not almost random, because 50 percent is almost heads or tails."


Well, mathematically a "perfect coin" has exactly that probability, 50%, and this time the minister surprises us with his command of the rudiments of [probability theory](http://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1lculo_de_probabilidades).

If Vidal's statement is correct, then flipping a coin is as good as applying the predictive model, and if the model was that bad, I wonder: are they only noticing now?

Maybe they've known for a while, but flipped a coin to decide whether to reveal the situation.
