A letter sent to El Mercurio by Dr. Horacio Croxatto and Professor María Elena
Ortiz of the Chilean Institute of Reproductive Medicine. I am copying it here
because El Mercurio's blogs tend to suffer from amnesia:

> Dear Editor:
>
> Following the ruling of the Constitutional Court (TC) on the
> so-called \"morning-after pill\", we feel bound to denounce
> that recital thirty-two of that ruling
> misuses our text, published (page 833) as part of
> chapter 27 of the book \"Selección de temas de Ginecoobstetricia II\".
> (eds) E. Guzmán, A.B. Lalonde. Editorial Publimpacto, Santiago, 2007.
> For your information and that of your readers, we transcribe
> both paragraphs below:
>
> 1.  Text in the TC ruling: \"\... specialists agree
>     that the mechanisms of action of the aforementioned emergency
>     contraception regimens involve three effects: a)
>     Preventing ovulation; b) Preventing the migration of
>     sperm to fertilize the egg, and c) Preventing
>     implantation (Horacio Croxatto A. and María Elena Ortiz.
>     \"Anticoncepción de emergencia con levonorgestrel\". In: Selección
>     de temas de Ginecoobstetricia II. Chapter 27 (eds.) E. Guzmán,
>     A.B. Lalonde. Editorial Publimpacto, Santiago, 2007, p. 833, and
>     Report of the Universidad Católica de Chile, p. 3)\".
>
> 2.  Our text in the cited Chapter 27: \"Most of the
>     research providing information on the mechanism of
>     action of EC with LNG was published in the first five years
>     of the 21st century. Before these studies only
>     hypotheses had been proposed, which can be summed up in three
>     main ones: that it interferes with the migration of
>     sperm, with ovulation, and with events after
>     fertilization, especially implantation. With no evidence
>     for or against, these hypotheses seeped into the literature and
>     made their way into textbooks, official documents of
>     the United States Food and Drug Administration
>     and of the pharmaceutical industry, and into the minds of many
>     professionals\".
>
> In the cited chapter and in other reviews on the subject, available
> to the TC, we have described and analyzed, objectively and clearly, the studies
> showing that, in women, the Cebus monkey and the rat, LNG
> given as an emergency contraceptive prevents pregnancy
> by interfering with processes prior to fertilization, and they lend no
> support whatsoever to the hypothesis that it might do so by interfering with
> the implantation of the embryo.
>
> As specialists in the subject, we do not agree that \"Preventing
> implantation\" is one of the mechanisms of action of the aforementioned
> emergency contraception regimens. We express our annoyance
> and dismay at the misuse of the quotation from our text, given that
> it was one of the arguments used as grounds for the TC's
> final decision.
>
> The inconsistency between what we published in our text and what the
> TC states in recital thirty-two of the ruling lays
> bare a transgression against the truth. We hold that to defend
> life it is not necessary to bend the truth, which must stand
> above any other consideration. Personal moral
> convictions do not justify a few imposing a moral dictatorship
> on our pluralistic society, even less so when it rests on
> distortions of the truth.
>
> Dr. Horacio B. Croxatto Prof. María Elena Ortiz\
> Chilean Institute of Reproductive Medicine
