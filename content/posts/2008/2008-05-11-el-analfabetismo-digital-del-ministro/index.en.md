Admitting ignorance can be a good thing. We appreciate it when someone
tells us in time that they are ignorant, because that timely honesty
lets us make a decision.

For example, if the person appointed as our children's history teacher
admits his ignorance of the subject, we would thank him, as long as the
admission came before he took the job. But if we find out afterward,
when a year of our children's education has been lost, that honesty is
of no use and earns no gratitude; on the contrary, it makes us deeply
indignant. (This situation is not as hypothetical as it sounds; it
happens all the time in our country's schools.)

Ignorance among the authorities is worrying, and I'm not talking about
a lack of specific technical knowledge; for that they have advisers and
a support team. What we all expect is that our authorities meet certain
minimum standards of competence. We expect them not to be functionally
illiterate, and to have some minimum preparation, fit for working in
the contemporary world.

That is why [the statements](http://replay.waybackmachine.org/20090115101619/http://www.elfrancotirador.cl/2008/05/11/la-frase-22/)
of the government's chief spokesman, the Minister Secretary General of
Government, are worrying:

> "I don't understand anything about computing, I barely know there's
> something called a hacker. I am a technological illiterate."

Because he says it with complete honesty, at ease, and without any
shame, almost as if it were charming, something that set him apart.
The minister should be ashamed.

But it worries me that he says it, because [what happened is very
serious](http://replay.waybackmachine.org/20090115101619/http://www.fayerwayer.com/2008/05/alerta-se-filtran-datos-personales-de-6-millones-de-chilenos-via-internet/).

A leak of the personal data of millions of Chileans is very grave,
all the more so because all that data comes from government
institutions.

It's true that our legislation and the state of personal data
protection in Chile are lamentable. There is even more data than what
was mentioned, circulating for a long time now, and nobody has done
anything. There are databases with sensitive information that can be
bought without any trouble, because deficient laws allow it!

The reason the authorities don't worry about the problems of protecting
identity data was confessed today, with complete honesty.

Our authorities don't understand that this is a serious problem. They
admit they are incapable of reading and understanding the implications
of the personal data of millions of Chileans leaking over the internet.

If the minister barely knows what a hacker is, forget about identity
theft.

Being illiterate means being unable to read and to understand, and
that is serious in a government authority. What do you think should be
done with an authority like that?

In Germany, not long ago, a group of hackers cloned [the interior
minister's
fingerprint](http://replay.waybackmachine.org/20090115101619/http://www.lnds.net/2008/04/la_huella_del_ministro.html).

Well, in Chile it is possible to clone the digital identity of our
Minister Secretary General, and the truth is it wouldn't take much.

Will we have to wait for a "proof of concept" to show up before the
minister realizes the cost of his digital illiteracy?

That's why they later sign [controversial
agreements](http://replay.waybackmachine.org/20090115101619/http://eldiabloenlosdetalles.net/acuerdo-marco-de-colaboracion-microsoft-gobierno-de-chile/);
it's pure digital illiteracy.
