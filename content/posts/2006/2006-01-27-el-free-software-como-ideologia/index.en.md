I've been asked to [clarify my position](/en/blog/lnds/2006/01/27/free-software-is-an-ideology/),
as if everything I've written in my time as a blogger weren't
enough.

Since I've always wanted to write at length about my view of libre
software, and about how it is in danger, I'm going to lay out my position
in a series of articles. Let's see if it stops being dismissed as
nonsense. Of course at the end I'll answer Ricardo Galli,
but I like to keep you in suspense :)

I want us to explore together what ethics, freedom,
ideologies, social conscience and participation are, concepts that tend to
get mixed up in discussions about libre software. Along the way
I may discover that I'm wrong, or on the contrary,
my view may prove to be right.

This is an exploration. An exploration of what libre software is
and the dangers it faces.

Stallman has always had an ideological position on what
free software should be, and he has never denied it. I separate free software from
libre software: to me free software is Stallman's ideology, and
libre software is something else, which we'll explore in the coming
articles.

## Ideology

> "Ideology" is defined as any form of explaining society
> that denies or excludes every other alternative way of explaining it; in this
> way it presents itself as a partial and disconnected worldview that:
> "Ignoring the multiple interconnections of the semantic universe,
> it also hides the practical reasons why certain
> signs were produced along with their interpretants. Thus forgetting
> produces a false consciousness".

<http://www.jorgemartinez.scd.cl/musunit.htm>

The author of this quote applies this definition in the context of the
need for musicology and its social usefulness. I chose the quote for
several reasons. The first is that it's on the site of the 
[Chilean copyright society](http://www.scd.cl/), which I think
should care a great deal about these issues. The second is that it is in the
field of artistic creation where the battle between the rights of
creators and of consumers comes to a head.\
The third reason is that the definition strikes me as right on target and
matches my view of what an ideology is.

Stallman would like everyone to share his view of what is the
right thing to do with software.\
So he invents the mechanisms, like the GPL, so that the licensing
system "traps" its users.

I share many of Stallman's premises, and I understand his
motivations, but that doesn't mean I have to accept everything he
says. Accepting without questioning is an ethical mistake, because I'm following the herd
without even asking myself whether what the herd is doing is right.

The idea of these articles is to explore the ethical problems I see
in Stallman's vision and how, deep down, it undermines
freedom. But for that I have to lay the foundations of my
arguments, so we first have to explore what ethics has to do with
all this.

I invite you to keep exploring tomorrow\....
