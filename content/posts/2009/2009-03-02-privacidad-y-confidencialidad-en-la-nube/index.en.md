Here's a [recent report](http://www.worldprivacyforum.org/pdf/WPF_Cloud_Privacy_Report.pdf)
(February 23) on the risks to privacy and confidentiality posed by
cloud computing.

The report, published by the [World Privacy Forum](http://www.worldprivacyforum.org/), identifies a series of
issues to consider in the context of cloud services.

For example, that in certain cases it could be illegal to hand data over
to a cloud service provider (in the United States), or that placing
certain information in the cloud may lower the legal barriers the
authorities would face to get at confidential personal information.

Interesting, because the WPF is worried about the way the cloud could
endanger privacy, and could break the symmetry of information
confidentiality in favor of the State, to the detriment of the citizen.

While leafing through this report, I couldn't help thinking about the
cloud services provided by the Chilean State. For example, the Servicio
de Impuestos Internos (the Chilean tax agency) has been providing cloud
services for quite a while (how convenient it is to issue your fee
invoice using the system they provide).

Well, for a long time now we've let our state, and other public or
semi-public institutions, handle a lot of information about us. The bad
part is that often this information isn't secured.

It would be interesting to contrast what the WPF lays out with the
Chilean reality, in the context of the cloud services the Chilean
government is implementing.
