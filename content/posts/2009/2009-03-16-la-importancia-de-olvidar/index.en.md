Apparently privacy isn't dead, it's just [a bit confused](http://www.sxsw.com/interactive/talks/panels/?action=show&id=IAP0901329).

One of the main threats to privacy is [the web's terrible capacity to remember](/en/blog/lnds/2009/02/04/digital-tattoos/).

As [Bruce Schneier says](http://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2009/02/privacy_in_the.html), in one of the clearest reflections on the problem we're going to face:

> Society works precisely because conversation is ephemeral; because people forget, and because people don't have to justify every word they utter.

So the key, it seems, is forgetting.

Like what [Google is doing](http://blog.wired.com/business/2009/03/googles-latitud.html) by giving up on keeping historical logs for its Google Latitude service. The police can't ask Google to hand over the information needed to determine whether a person took part in a political rally in a given public square.

This is interesting, and it's a good thing. These new "networked social services" must have the ability to forget. It's a good way to protect people's privacy.
