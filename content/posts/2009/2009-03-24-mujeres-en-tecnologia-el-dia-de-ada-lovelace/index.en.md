Today is [Ada Lovelace](http://www.pledgebank.com/AdaLovelaceDay) Day, an initiative that
seeks to highlight the role of women in technology, and
[just as we had pledged](/en/blog/lnds/2009/01/17/the-first-systems-analyst/),
here is my contribution to the occasion.

Ada Lovelace was the first programmer. Today you'll be able to read a
lot about this extraordinary woman, her contribution to history, and
about [many important women in our field](http://ada.pint.org.uk/language.php?language=Spanish), like
[Grace Hopper](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Hopper), or [Adele
Goldberg](http://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/muiseum/goldberg/goldberg_page.htm),
or [Barbara Liskov](http://blogs.lug.fi.uba.ar/marga/?title=evolutiva&more=1&c=1&tb=1&pb=1). I invite you to read about them.

I currently work at a company where women have a significant presence
in technology, but it hasn't always been like that. Either way, the
presence of women in our field has been growing, fortunately.

![mcrivara.jpeg](mcrivara.jpeg)

In this first
year of Ada Lovelace Day, I think it's fitting to start with a small
tribute to one of my teachers at the computer science department of the
Universidad de Chile, [María Cecilia Rivara](http://www.dcc.uchile.cl/~mcrivara/).

María Cecilia is one of the top-level researchers in her area, but I
remember her because she got us interested in computer graphics, in the
late eighties and early nineties, with her SIGGRAPH videos, where we got
to see some groundbreaking animations by the people who would become the
kings of the field, like the folks at Pixar.

But above all, she was a professor devoted to the well-being of her
students. I remember her especially because she was head of the
department when I studied there, and she helped and guided me at certain
moments of my time as a student. In the end, you remember your teachers
more for their human qualities than for the knowledge they pass on to
you.

Greetings to María Cecilia, and through her to all the women colleagues
I work with, and have worked with. Forgive me for publishing so late,
but this is the first year; let's hope next year we can have more
people taking part.

And you, who would you honor on [Ada Lovelace Day](http://findingada.com/) ?
