I don't know if you knew this, but the first systems analyst, and
programmer, was a woman: Countess [Ada Lovelace](http://www.sdsc.edu/ScienceWomen/lovelace.html), daughter of the famous English poet Lord Byron.

She studied the designs of the machine Charles Babbage wanted to
build, the famous Analytical Engine. She understood Babbage's device so
well that she could articulate the promise of this new machine much
better than its inventor. She realized that it was what we would call
today a general-purpose computer, capable of
"developing and tabulating any function whatever... the engine may be
described as being the material expression of any indefinite function
of any degree of generality and complexity".

What's all this about?

This coming March 24 will be [Ada Lovelace
Day](http://www.pledgebank.com/AdaLovelaceDay), a day on which
several of us have pledged to write about Ada Lovelace and reflect on
the role of women in computing. It's an idea driven by
[Suw Charman-Anderson](http://suw.org.uk/about-me),
co-author of [Strange Attractor](http://strange.corante.com/). So I'll see you on March 24, when we commemorate this great woman.
