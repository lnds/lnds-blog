> There are an endless pile of tools engineers are fond of using in their
development process, but there are only
four that they really need:
>
> - Editor
> - Compiler
> - Version control
> - Bug tracking
>
> -- Michael "Rands" Lopp

![](programadores.jpg)

Everyone who develops software for a living has some kind of
editor or integrated development environment (IDE) and a compiler
available.

The incredible thing is that version control and a bug
tracking tool are missing at many companies whose business is
developing software.

A while ago we ran into a vendor that had an important
piece of software we needed, but before long we realized
they didn't have these basic practices in place, and because of that they couldn't
meet the demands of our business. So we decided to
guide them into using them. We asked them to adopt
version control, and we even added them to our own
"Jira" so we could track their bugs and pending tasks.
These are minimal practices, and in this case they helped bring order to the
project and are helping to improve the final product. I also think
it has helped that company internally, both to improve its
products and services and to value its technical team more, because
we went straight to the executive staff to tell them
they had to implement these practices in their engineering team. Although
it was a slow process, the results are starting to show.

All of this, of course, is a necessary condition but not a
sufficient one, I have to clarify. What matters is that adopting these
basic tools gives you a minimal foundation for controlling the
complexity of any project.

You have to control the chaos of managing your projects, and these
four simple things will do a lot for you.
As [Michael "Rands" Lopp says](http://www.randsinrepose.com/archives/2004/07/10/what_to_do_when_youre_screwed.html):

> These tools empower engineers by allowing
the entire team to:
>
> -   Collaborate without stepping on each others toes. You do this, I'll
    do that.
> -   Be accountable for their work. Who's got that checked out? Who's
    bug is this anyway?
> -   Measure their work. How many bugs do I have? How about
    you?
> -   Remember what they did. Uh, who the hell checked in this crap?
>
> You'll find early on in your management stint that
main reason people make the trek to your office is that they need conflict
resolution. Once the conflict participants stop yelling,
you need to get them looking at facts because facts will ground them and
grounded means less yelling. All of the tools I describe above are excellent
repositories of cold hard facts and that can help.

As an added bonus, and if you care about this business of
certifications, I can tell you that according to the honorable Software
Engineering Institute (SEI), having these four simple
things can earn you a lovely CMMI Level 2 certification
("at this level organizations have
institutionalized project management practices, there are some basic
metrics and a reasonable tracking of quality. The relationship with
subcontractors and clients is managed systematically").

Do you have these four basic tools? How many
institutions do you know that aren't even using version
control? Unfortunately, there are many more than you would suspect.
