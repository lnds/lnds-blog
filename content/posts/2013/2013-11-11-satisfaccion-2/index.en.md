We live in times when people do not know how to appreciate nature, what
they have, the wonder of life. This morning someone in my
Twitter timeline wrote the following:

> Yesterday I went to a tourist spot where people were frantically
> taking pictures of themselves for "face"---contemplating the natural beauty was
> an afterthought -- \@juque
> <https://twitter.com/juque/status/399876149959876608>
{{<x user="juque" id="399876149959876608">}}

We live in times of appearances, where what seems to matter is
looking successful. It doesn't matter if you are standing before a wonder of
nature, if you are in front of Torres del Paine, or
Niagara Falls. What people care about is proving to their
peers, to their "friends," that they were there, posting it on their Facebook
wall. Being shaken by the sheer scale of nature no longer matters.
Five minutes of photos and that's it, on to the next thing.

I don't know if I pursue success. I am not clear on what it means. Winning at
something? Earning some medal? I am old school. I give more weight to the
satisfaction I get from doing something, sharing with someone, living a
moment.

There is an old adage about planting a tree, having a child and writing a
book as signs of achievement, symbols of total fulfillment as a person,
as a man. In this success-centered age that gets read as
success in life, the ultimate accomplishment. Surely nobody stops to consider the
message that carrying out those three acts actually conveys.

It is about transcendence. We want, we need to transcend. Even those
who chase success, or fame, are seeking transcendence, in a
twisted, meaningless way. Others stop seeking transcendence
because they settle for the hollow imitation that religion offers them, or
belief in some god. All of them have lost the sense of transcending. That is
why in the end they feel no satisfaction, or they look for it in things that don't
matter, or that simply don't exist.

Today I turn forty-seven, and I feel satisfied with my life.
Some will see it as successful, others will find it dull, or nerdy. I don't
measure it in either of those ways, but I do feel it has been an
interesting, full, satisfying life. Being with the people who love me, feeling
that they love me, and feeling the affection of so many other people beyond
my inner circle means I am having a full life, that I am
fulfilling that mandate of the universe: to transcend.

> "I want to put a ding in the universe -- Steve Jobs

I have a long way to go, there is still so much left to do. I hope to have the strength and
the health to keep going. In the last few weeks I have done many
things, from taking part in conferences to
[publishing a book](/books/)! And now it is my birthday, and all I
can feel is great satisfaction with my life, with my family, with my
friends and acquaintances.

I invite you to enjoy life's satisfactions, to value
what you have, to worry less about success, or about living a saintly life.
To live! As Nietzsche says, "The maturity of man—that means, to have reacquired
the seriousness that one had as a child at play." Let's recover
that seriousness of the child. Let's give ourselves over to play. As Cioran said: "if
we are a mere accident, why take it all so seriously?"

The next time you are somewhere beautiful, enjoy it. Before
taking the photo to post on "face," hold your partner close,
tell them you love them. Kiss your children, and if you don't have any,
have them! What you are really looking for is not happiness, not
success, it is transcendence. When you have reached it, leaving your
seed in the hearts of your descendants, when you have touched the
soul of your fellow human beings, you will find satisfaction.
