Just like that, without my noticing, this blog has turned eight, or
2 to the power of 3, for fans of binary math.

That is quite a while, and not common. There are few of us persistent
bloggers left. It is true that lately I have been writing
less often, but we are still here, and what matters is that
many of you, my reader friends, are still here too,
keeping me company. Thank you very much for your patience.

This blog started on August 1, 2005 with a very simple
[post](/blog/2005/08/desarrollar-software-es-como-hacer-una-pelicula.html)
that began like this:

> **Developing software is like making a movie**
>
> Art+Technique+Logistics

> Just as in the making of a film, when we build software
we have a Pre-Production stage, where we write the script. This is
equivalent to the requirements analysis and design stage.
>
>Then comes the production stage, when the whole crew, under the
director's guidance, works on shooting the film's scenes and
sequences. In software development we do the same: the project
manager coordinates the team of programmers.
>
>In film, Post-Production comes next: editing, selecting or
discarding scenes, finishing special effects, adding the music,
etc. In software we have to test, approve, fix, polish,
run the tests, integrate, test in production environments,
prepare the operations manuals, define contingencies, etc.
>
>Although there are similarities, the film industry has many more
lessons for us, and in a way they do a much more professional job than
many software developers.

Eight years have gone by and many of those ideas are still present in this
space. What's more, last year I met a friend who told me he
found this blog precisely through that post. This blog has let me
meet many fellow computing people, and also people involved in
technology and entrepreneurship.

This blog is my space for personal reflection. It is my way of learning
new things and presenting them to you. I have had a lot of fun
doing it, and I hope you have enjoyed some of the
articles.

As always on these occasions, a toast is in order: cheers to you
and to La Naturaleza del Software.
