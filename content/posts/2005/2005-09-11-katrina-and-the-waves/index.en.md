[Katrina and the waves](http://www.katw.com/). The name of this
eighties band sounds today like a strange premonition, one of life's
little curiosities.

But the truth is that Hurricane Katrina has made waves.

I recently wrote that there are no [natural disasters](/en/blog/lnds/2005/09/09/natural-disasters/),
that they are instead consequences of policy. And I also talked about
global warming.

Reading the post, it actually looks as if I were drawing a
direct link between global warming and Hurricane Katrina.\
That was not my intention. As
[Carlos](http://cdp.blogsome.com/2005/09/11/katrina-y-el-cambio-climatico-global/) rightly points out,
we cannot establish a direct link and say that Hurricane Katrina
has anything to do with global warming. Although we can't
rule it out either.

What I wanted to highlight in that article was the political side. On the radio
I heard the director of [Chile's ONEMI](http://www.onemi.cl/) (the
National Emergency Office) say that there really are no **natural
catastrophes**. Of course: if nobody lived in the Mississippi delta we
wouldn't be talking about this. When a natural
phenomenon coincides with human settlements in a place, that is when
these misnamed natural catastrophes happen. But these
catastrophes can have greater or lesser impact; it is a matter of
organization, resources, early warnings, coordination, and so on. That is,
the effects of these phenomena can be mitigated, or made worse, by
political decisions. A good analysis of this can be read in
[this
article](http://laotraorilla.blog-city.com/el_verdadero_rostro_de_la_tragedia.htm)
from [La Otra Orilla](http://laotraorilla.blog-city.com/).
