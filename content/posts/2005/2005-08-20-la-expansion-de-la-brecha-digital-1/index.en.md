The famous [Hubble's Law](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble's_law)
states that the speed at which galaxies move away from each other is
proportional to their distance.

Something similar happens with the so-called [digital divide](http://www.brechadigital.org/).

Let me reason through a few simple examples.

Ringtones for cell phones are becoming popular these days (a ringtone
costs between 1 and 3 dollars on average, and maybe more if it's
polyphonic). Most people are moving from the old cell phone, with no
way to change the ringtone, to buying phones with monophonic ringtones,
but the more privileged have access to phones with polyphonic
ringtones. And some of us, \"luckier\" still, can use an MP3 of our
favorite artist instead of little tunes synthesized in MIDI.\
Meanwhile some of us can get cell phones with a 128 MB MMC memory card,
and we can download MP3s and send them over bluetooth to our phones, or
thanks to USB adapters we can use the MMC as a removable disk, without
paying the carriers a single peso for it (they fleece us somewhere
else, of course).

That is the digital divide

Worse still: some time from now, when phones with MP3 players and
external MMC memory are common and are given away with cell phone plans
(if those still exist down the road), it's likely that, if luck keeps
smiling on us, we will have some other technology that lets us
communicate with any part of the world through a subvocalizer, or
through a \"phone\" that will probably be a millimeter across, with
room for who knows how many terabytes of data to store enough music
that we will never get around to listening to.

That is the digital divide.

The cheapest cell phones don't auto-lock their keypad, which causes
their users to make calls by accident, spending money from their plan
or from their prepaid cards. If you buy a high-end phone, on the other
hand, you get automatic keypad lock, and you will never spend a cent on
unwanted calls.

That is the digital divide.

Today a low-priced personal computer is being offered on credit,
payable over 36 months. According to Moore's law, by the time someone
finishes paying for their poor PC, the market will have machines with
at least 4 times the storage and processing capacity.

That is the digital divide.

It turns out that in the last 2 weeks 10,000 PCs have been sold through
the MPPC campaign, and in that same time the MPPCDV campaign has
gathered the same number of signatures. (Source:
<http://aguayoki.cl/eduardo/>**2005**/08/20/diez-mil/)

That is the digital divide

But with every month that goes by this divide gets bigger, and some
time from now those of us on the digital side will be moving at a speed
proportional to the distance that those at the non-digital end will
have to cover.
