Reading the blog of 
[Fernando Flores - Abriendo Juegos, Abriendo Mundos](http://www.fernandoflores.cl/node/523) I discovered that they titled
the xml feed **Sindicalizar**.

That made me wonder, so I looked it up in the [RAE](http://www.rae.es/)
dictionary, and it turns out *sindicalizar* is not in the dictionary!!!

What to do with this neologism? Especially since sindicalizar (to
unionize) has other connotations in Spanish (or Castilian?).

It looks ugly on Mr. Flores's site.\
So I set out on an investigation\....

The word *sindicar* does exist in our language, but it has the following
meanings:

> 1.  tr. to accuse (ǁ to denounce).
> 2.  tr. To mark with a note, blemish or suspicion.
> 3.  tr. To subject a sum of money or a certain class of securities or
>     goods to special commitments in order to trade or sell them.
> 4.  tr. To bind together several people of the same profession, or
>     with common interests, to form a union.
> 5.  prnl. To join a union.

([Source: RAE](http://www.rae.es/)).

The [Webster](http://www.webster.com/) dictionary led me to the
following definition for Syndicate:

> Function: verb Inflected Form(s): -cat·ed; -cat·ing transitive senses
> 1 : to subject to or manage as a syndicate 2 a : to sell (as a
> cartoon) to a syndicate or for publication in many newspapers or
> periodicals at once b : to sell (as a series of television programs)
> directly to local stations

Source:
<http://www.webster.com/cgi-bin/dictionary?book=Dictionary&va=syndication&x=10&y=11>.

From there I jumped in turn to [Britanica.com](http://ww.britanica.com/), where
I found other, shadier meanings :)

**syndicate**

> in the United States, an association of racketeers in control of
> organized crime (q.v.).

(source:
<http://www.britannica.com/search?query=syndicate&submit=Find&source=MWTEXT>)

**newspaper syndicate**

> also called Press Syndicate, or Feature Syndicate, agency that sells
to newspapers and other media special writing and artwork, often written
by a noted journalist or eminent authority or drawn by a well-known
cartoonist, that cannot be classified as spot coverage of the news. Its
fundamental service is to spread the cost of expensive features among as
many newspapers (subscribers) as possible. Press syndicates\...

(source:
<http://www.britannica.com/eb/article-9055610?query=syndicate&ct=>)

But everything becomes clearer with this lovely WikiPedia article:\
<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndicate>

It turns out the word in question has its origin in the Greek
*syndikos*, which is something like the person in charge of a matter, an
**[ombusdam](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ombudsman)**.

The word **Syndication**, on the other hand, is according to wikipedia a
method for making content available to a wide variety of outlets (points
of sale or redistribution) simultaneously.

The great examples of this means of distribution are the cartoonists,
such as those distributed by [United Feature Syndicate,
Inc.](http://www.unitedmedia.com/), who \"syndicate\" Snoopy and
Dilbert, among others.

This investigation leads me to think that the proper thing would be to
use the phrase *Sindicación Web* for the act of publishing part or all
of a website's content to be used by other sites. It reads better, and
it comes close to the concept of [Web
Syndication](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_syndication) defined in
Wikipedia.

Finally, you can do what I do on my blog: just put up the little orange
badge with the 3 letters XML, and that's it, a blogger knows what it is
about.
