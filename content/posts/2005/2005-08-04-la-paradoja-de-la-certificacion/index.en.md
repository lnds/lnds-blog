I'll start with a **real-life story**

A young guy asked me which certification program would be a good one to
take, and I told him to go for the DCE, for the simple reason that
besides the training it is a showcase for getting noticed by potential
employers. In fact, after earning his third star he was contacted about
a good job and he is switching for a better salary :smile: Happy ending.

## Background

Microsoft's DCE program is free
<http://www.microsoft.com/spanish/msdn/comunidad/dce/default.asp>, and
the Empower ISV <https://partner.microsoft.com/global/40010429> has an
annual cost of US$ 500, with which Microsoft is betting on growing its
developer base.

One advantage of the DCE is that it is a showcase to present yourself as
a developer and land a job. The bar is high: getting a third star takes
time, study, dedication.\
In any case, if you want higher levels of certification you have to pay
around US$ 100 per exam.

By contrast, RedHat offers its RHCT programs at a cost of US$ 750 or
more <https://www.redhat.com/training/rhce/courses/>.    


For Java I looked, but nothing was clear to me because their site shows
nothing
<http://cl.sun.com/service/education/certification/java/java_certpath.html>.

Now comes the paradox

If open source is funded by services, the natural thing is that to get
certified in Linux I have to pay for a course and pay for the exam (as
redhat does). It is true that I can learn linux on my own or for free,
but most people want to take a proper course, and that is a good revenue
stream for companies built on open source.

The paradox is that companies built on proprietary software, like MS,
can give the training away, because they want more developers using
their technologies, to get users and programmers hooked.

If people are motivated by improving their income, they will end up
choosing the microsoft path, since getting certified costs less.

Open source is trapped by its services model in this respect, because it
has to charge for training and certifying professionals, and
professionals will take the path of least cost.
