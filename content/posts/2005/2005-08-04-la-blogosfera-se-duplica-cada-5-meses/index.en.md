The blogosphere doubles every 5 months!!! Wow!\
Source: [http://www.fernandoflores.cl/node/523](http://replay.waybackmachine.org/20060211180549/http://www.fernandoflores.cl/node/523)

It seems growth always looks like this, mysteriously analogous to
[Moore's Law](http://replay.waybackmachine.org/20060211180549/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moores_Law).

Could we be looking at a case of [Moron's Law](http://replay.waybackmachine.org/20060211180549/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moron%27s_law),
which tells us that the number of incorrect observations of Moore's Law
doubles every 18 months?

What really matters is the level of activity. According to technorati,
55% of blogs remain active, a statistic that has held steady over a
year. 13% are updated weekly.

(source: [http://www.sifry.com/alerts/archives/000332.html](http://replay.waybackmachine.org/20060211180549/http://www.sifry.com/alerts/archives/000332.html))

Hmmm\...\
Let's see\...

According to [Sifry](http://replay.waybackmachine.org/20060211180549/http://www.sifry.com/alerts/), we have one post every 10.4 seconds, and there are 14.2 million blogs as
of August 2005.

According to [Internet World Stats](http://replay.waybackmachine.org/20060211180549/http://www.internetworldstats.com/),
the world population is 6,420,102,722 people, and internet penetration
is 14.6%, which means we have 938,710,929\
internet users.

The number of bloggers registered on technorati represents 1.51% of
internet users (assuming 1 blog per user, which is not my case, because
I have more than 1 blog and all of them are registered on technorati).

That is no small thing\...

There is still room to grow. I would like to know how much information
this noisy 1.51% produces, and using information theory we could
calculate how much quality there is in blogs. So I am eagerly waiting
for more statistics\....
