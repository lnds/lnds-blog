The law of the expansion of the digital divide is as follows:

Vt = H x Dt

Where Vt is the speed at which the divide expands, H is a constant (to
be determined) and Dt is the size of the divide.

Don't take it too seriously. I'm just abusing Hubble's law, applying it
as a way to highlight the fact that the divide keeps getting bigger
over time.

Being absolutely and knowingly non-rigorous, I'm going to amuse myself
speculating with this law.

If the size of the divide today is 100 \[u\], and H is 1.11 \[1/month\]
(I'll explain this number in a moment), the speed of expansion of the
divide is 1.1 \* 100, that is, 110 \[u/month\].

In 18 months, the digital divide will have grown by 110\*18, that is,
an additional 1980 \[u\].

I picked the value 1.11 on the grounds that if Moore's Law is true,
then every 18 months technological capacity doubles. So I said: if my
capacity today is 1, that capacity reaches 2 in 18 months, which means
it increases by 2/18 every month. (I'm abusing it, I insist).

A young person who buys a computer on 36-month credit, and has X \[u\]
of digital divide, will have accumulated 3960 + X \[u\] of divide by
the time he stops paying.

The worst part is that by then the more advanced world will be moving
at 4356 \[u/month\] relative to him.

It gives me chills to think that someone smart, and with the ability,
might pick up this speculative reasoning and discover there is
something of value behind it\...

Please criticize this reasoning, because it scares me\...
