I'm picking up the comments from
[Francotirador](http://www.argonave.com/francotirador/) and from
[Carlos](http://cdp.blogsome.com/).

As [Godo](http://godomuro.blogspot.com/) would say in his sarcastic
tone, it's good for the ego to know people are reading you :).

It's true that we have to change the **somebody has to** into **we
have to**. But since a blog is a historical record, I won't change it
in the original; I'll fix the mistake in this post instead.

I'm not talking about going out to protest with our laptops in front of
La Moneda, the presidential palace, because I don't think that's the
smartest way to get attention.

I have a confession to make. A few days ago I met Carlos Alvarez,
undersecretary of economy, our national CIO, on the occasion of an
award I received. The context and the timing weren't right to bring up
the subject. I'm not a friend or an acquaintance of his, but he doesn't
strike me as someone with bad intentions or eager to cut deals with the
big department stores or microsoft. I think it's a problem of not
knowing, of wanting to do something quick and flashy. I don't know his
motivations, but from the little I got to talk with him (it was about
another subject, entrepreneurship), he is an economist very much driven
by figures, by achieving numerical impact. I may be wrong, but your
profession ends up warping you.

This whole problem probably starts from the belief, mistaken in my
view, that the digital divide shrinks if we have more people using PCs
and connected to the internet. (I'll talk about this later)

I think the kind of action we need to take in the \"unconnected
world\", or real world, is to build ties with the decision makers, get
to talk with them and explain why we believe they're making the wrong
decisions. There are two paths: we go out and burn buses in the
streets, comrades, or we make use of whatever first- or second-degree
contacts we may have with the authorities.

I know people who know Carlos Alvarez, and I think my recent meeting
with him would let me approach him, so as a concrete action I propose
trying to set up an appointment with him.

I don't think the MPPC project should be killed, because there are
people who will only get access to a computer by using their store
credit card, but I do think the program has to be complemented and
expanded, following the guidelines of the digital agenda itself. People
have to be given options. If they want to use Microsoft, fine, XP isn't
bad, but XPLE is an abomination :). But let's show them they can save
some CLP 50,000 by using other operating systems. That they can even
buy better hardware with that money. How many small and medium-sized
businesses that import and assemble PCs would benefit if they had the
advertising support the big stores get from the MPPC program.

Some of you probably already have first-degree access to Carlos
Alvarez. I'm offering to try on my side, but maybe we should coordinate
an effort to meet with the authorities. This movement has no head, and
nobody is trying to be the leader or anything, but if a bunch of us
show up in front of Alvarez, with the backing of this adhocratic group
that has formed, with the specific weight each of us carries, I think
we can get something done.

Finally, if we aren't heard, nobody will be able to accuse us of never
having left the glass towers that are our blogs.

If I get a contact, I'll let you know.
