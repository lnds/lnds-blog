Cuba, best Ibero-American country at the International Olympiad in
Informatics

Havana, 1 (AIN) The Cubans Daniel Rodríguez Sánchez, with a silver
medal, and David Cuellar Morales, with a bronze, made Cuba the best
country in Ibero-America at the International Olympiad in Informatics
held in Poland.

The event drew 276 competitors from 76 nations, and the young men from
the Island finished with satisfactory results, considering the high
level of their rivals, the newspaper Juventud Rebelde notes today.

Over two days of competition Daniel and David each solved six problems
of an algorithmic nature, for which they wrote software using a
programming language.

The former scored 418 points out of a possible 600, good for silver,
and the latter won the bronze with a total of 335.

The coach and teacher Francisco Hernández pointed out that China and
Slovakia came out with four gold medals each, while in Ibero-America
Brazil, with two bronzes, came next after Cuba.

Daniel Rodríguez and David Cuellar Morales finished twelfth grade at
the Instituto Preuniversitario Vocacional de Ciencias Exactas
Comandante Ernesto Che Guevara, in Villa Clara, and they are satisfied
with the results, a real achievement considering the high level of
their rivals.

The Cuban preselection was put together from the results of the
National Computing Contest for upper secondary level, and the students
receive training while their development of skills in solving typical
olympiad exercises is evaluated.

The strong performance of the two from Villa Clara allowed the Island,
for the third year in a row, to remain among the standout countries in
competitions of this kind, since in each edition they won one silver
and one bronze medal.

Cuba has taken part in every olympiad in the discipline except the one
held in 2003 in the United States, because that country denied visas to
the Island's delegation.
