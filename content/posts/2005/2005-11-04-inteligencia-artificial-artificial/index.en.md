There is a famous story about a robot shaped like a Turk that played
chess in the late 18th century[^1].
The truth is that behind the robot there was a person who was
actually doing the playing.

That idea is what inspires the name of Amazon's new project:

[Amazon Mechanical Turk: Artifical Artifical Inteligence](https://www.mturk.com/mturk/welcome)

The idea is that Amazon will pay for tasks that only humans can
perform, the so-called HITs (Human Intelligence Tasks).

Things as simple as: how many chessboards do you see in this
image?

![turco.jpg](turco.jpg)

A new way to earn a few cents by improving the information
Amazon handles.\
Thousands of people working behind the scenes, creating the illusion that there is
a great artificial intelligence.\
An interesting model. Remember that the value of information
businesses will be determined by the
[relevance of that information](/en/blog/lnds/2005/11/03/does-it-matter-whether-software-is-open/), and
since we don't have artificial intelligence yet, we can fake it.

Clever\....

## Fortune or glory

In fact, a HIT can be transcribing or summarizing a book, or translating it.

One of the foundations of what is being called Web 2.0 is giving
power and participation to the user, and this is one of the most
powerful examples.

Want to do innovative business in Web 2.0? Get your users
to participate. Bring them into your business by motivating them to take part,
whether for fortune (like Amazon) or for glory (like Wikipedia).
