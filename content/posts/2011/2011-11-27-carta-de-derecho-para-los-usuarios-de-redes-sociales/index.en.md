In March of this year, in the wake of the Google and Facebook incidents
late last year, which showed how these companies, and
probably many more, do not respect their users' privacy, a
group of panelists at the [SXSW](http://sxsw.com/) conference in the
United States drafted a document they called the [Social Network
Users' Bill of Rights](http://schedule.sxsw.com/events/event_IAP7315).

This bill of rights hasn't gotten much attention, as far as I know, but it is
interesting, and important. I think we users of social networks
should push for the creation of a document like this one, with
real legal weight, don't you think? Here is how the
document opens, and I look forward to your opinions.


> *We the users expect social network sites to provide us the following rights
> in their Terms of Service, Privacy Policies, and implementations of their system:*
>
> 1.  *Honesty: Honor your privacy policy and terms of
>     service*
>
> 2.  *Clarity: Make sure that policies, terms of service, and
>     settings are easy to find and understand*
>
> 3.  *Freedom of speech: Do not delete or modify my data without a
>     clear policy and justification*

The document goes on to list fourteen rights in all. Via [Identity Blog.](http://www.identityblog.com/?p=1172)
