The Spanish version of this post is my translation of an
[article](http://cacm.acm.org/magazines/2010/1/55760-what-should-we-teach-new-software-developers-why/fulltext)
published by Bjarne Stroustrup in Communications of the ACM in January
2010. [Bjarne Stroustrup](http://www2.research.att.com/~bs/) is the
creator of the C++ language. Here is a short excerpt of the original.

# What should we teach new software developers? Why?


by **Bjarne Stroustrup.**

Published in Communications of the ACM ([original text](http://cacm.acm.org/magazines/2010/1/55760-what-should-we-teach-new-software-developers-why/fulltext))\
Vol. 53 No. 1, Pages 40-42\
10.1145/1629175.1629192

> Computer science must be at the center of software systems
> development. If it is not, we must rely on individual experience and
> rules of thumb, ending up with less capable, less reliable systems,
> developed and maintained at unnecessarily high cost. We need changes
> in education to allow for improvements of industrial practice.

![](Eraser.jpg)

The article covers the problem (the disconnect between computer science
education and what industry needs), why it matters even if your
computer hasn't crashed lately, the academia/industry gap, and dreams
of professionalism, before reaching its conclusion.

{{<figure caption="Bjarne Stroustrup" src="Bjarne-300x218.jpg">}}

![](chasm.jpg)

Stroustrup closes by suggesting a structure of CS education based on a
core plus specializations and application areas, aiming eventually at
licensing(1) of software artifacts and of at least some of the
professionals who produce them. The rest is in the 
[original text](http://cacm.acm.org/magazines/2010/1/55760-what-should-we-teach-new-software-developers-why/fulltext).

**Bjarne Stroustrup** is the College of Engineering Chair in Computer
Science Professor at Texas A&M University in College Station, TX.

Notes:

\(1\) To understand why Stroustrup says the subject of licensing is
controversial I suggest reading [this document](http://www.cse.unsw.edu.au/~se4921/PDF/CACM/p91-white.pdf).
Licensing refers to the certification of professionals by some body so
that they can practice, as happens with lawyers and the supreme court,
or with professional associations in various countries.

\(2\) In the Spanish translation I used the term "computación" where
the author used computer science (CS), and kept the original term in
some paragraphs to try to preserve the meaning of the text.

No machine translator was used for the Spanish translation. It probably
has many errors; I will appreciate your comments and improvements.
