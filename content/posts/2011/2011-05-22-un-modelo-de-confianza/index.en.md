Trust can be visualized with this equation

> **T = (C + R + I) / S**

Where the terms are:

-   **T**: Trust, which is the result.

-   **C**: Credibility, which comes from credentials, or knowledge, plus
    honesty.

-   **R**: Reliability, dependability, knowing that promises are kept.

-   **I**: Intimacy, which touches the emotions; it is feeling connected
    to the other person, it is openness, transparency.

-   **S**, Self orientation or Selfishness, the focus on your own
    goals at the expense of the goals of the relationship.

Notice that the first factors, credibility, reliability and intimacy, add
up to trust. They are gradual: each element on its own helps increase
trust. But as selfishness grows, self-interest, the wish to push your own
agenda at the expense of the common goal, the value of trust drops fast.
The more selfishness, the less trust.

You can analyze any relationship with this model. One way would be to
assign values from 1 to 10 to each of these parameters; in that case a
relationship of perfect trust should give you a value of 30, and absolute
distrust would have a value of 0.3.

Some tips for improving these parameters (taken from
[here](http://www.collieassociates.com/common/Trust_Equation.pdf)):

**CREDIBILITY**

-   Tell the truth

-   Don't exaggerate

-   When you don't know something, say so quickly and directly

-   Do your homework

-   Love what you do

**RELIABILITY**

-   Make small commitments, on small things, and keep them

-   Use the terminology of your client, or user

-   Be consistent

-   Trust links intentions to actions

**INTIMACY**

-   Intimacy comes with time

-   Open up your process, be transparent

-   You have to know how to listen

-   Show that you care about the other person

**SELFISHNESS**

-   Let your counterpart fill in those blanks in a conversation

-   Listen, be reflective

-   Focus on defining the problem, not on describing the solution

-   Take responsibility for failures in communication

-   Be honest with yourself: if you have little interest in this
    situation, it is almost inevitable that you will focus more on
    yourself than on achieving joint success

-   It's about committing, with the greatest intensity, to helping the
    other person (your client).
