Responsible: "Obliged to answer for something or for someone".

The suffix 'able', like 'ible', turns a verb into an adjective that denotes
"the capacity or possibility of". So from irritate comes irritable, capable
of being irritated, or variable, which comes from vary and means having the
capacity to vary, to change. And so responsible comes from respond, that
is, capable of responding.

When a leader says that nobody has the responsibility, we have a serious
problem. Because part of leadership is establishing responsibilities. This
person is in charge, and therefore responsible: the one who must answer for
what happens, the one who must know what is going on in order to report it
to the leader.

On the other hand, the person responsible has to know what to answer for.
Being responsible does not mean having to do all the work, as many believe.
What's more, the work may be carried out by others, and you are still
responsible. He is the one who answers: if somebody asks how things are
going, he has to know where they stand, even if he isn't the one doing
them.

The design of responsibilities is crucial, and we neglect it so often. We
don't define responsibilities or who is responsible, we don't define what
we must answer for, or to whom, and that's how the problems start.
