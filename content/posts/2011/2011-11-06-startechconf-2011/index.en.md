I came away very happy from [StartechConf 2011](http://www.startechconf.com/). I have to congratulate its organizing
team.

{{<figure caption="with \@stefsull and \@garazi" src="startechconf.jpg">}}

It was 2 very intense days of high-quality talks, and the
guests were top-notch. We're talking about speakers who are
extremely well known on the worldwide development conference
circuit.

I think everyone who attended learned a lot. Some of you,
dear readers (those using modern browsers), will notice that
I picked up a bit of CSS3 after the excellent talks by the couple
formed by [Stephanie Sullivan Rewis](http://www.w3conversions.com/)
([\@stefsull](https://twitter.com/#!/stefsull) on Twitter) and her husband
Greg Rewis ([\@garazi](https://twitter.com/#!/garazi) on Twitter).

It's true that most of the talks leaned toward
front-end development (HTML5, CSS3, jQuery, etc.). But there was also
something for the back end, and we had a very good conceptual talk on
relational and non-relational databases given by [Mark Ramm](http://compoundthinking.com/blog/)
([\@markramm](https://twitter.com/#!/markramm)), a talk I
paid special attention to, since it's one of the topics keeping me busy
these days. While I'm at it, let me tell you that this Thursday we're holding a
[MeetUp of the Dcc8090 group](http://www.meetup.com/dcc8090/) together with Ubaldo
Taladriz (\@utaladriz) at the Colegio de Ingenieros, a talk on
non-relational databases where we'll share our experiences. We'll
be expecting you on Thursday at 7:30 PM; you can sign up
[here](http://www.meetup.com/dcc8090/events/39723222/) and there will be streaming
on this very blog, in case you can't make it.

Personally, the 2 talks I liked most, mainly because
they were given by people I admire for what they're doing, were the by now
classic A Tale of Three Trees, by Scott Chacon, who surprised us
by giving it in perfect Spanish, presenting it as [Un Cuento de Tres
Arboles](http://speakerdeck.com/u/schacon/p/un-cuento-de-tres-arboles).

{{<figure caption="With Scott Chacon" src="with-chacon.jpg">}}

Who is Scott Chacon? Well, he's the author of [the book Pro Git](http://progit.org/book/), from which several of us learned to use
this tool. Let's say that with this photo I'm 1 degree of separation
from Linus Torvalds (creator of Linux and of course of Git), because Scott
maintains the official site of the [Git community](http://git-scm.com/).
By pure chance we ended up sitting next to each other during Greg
Rewis's talk, and we shared a few minutes. That's part of what
this kind of conference gives you: the chance to meet great
developers and, of course, to do some "networking", as the
pretentious crowd likes to say.

I made the arrangements to take my team of developers to the
event, and my company not only paid for our tickets,
it also made it easy for them to attend without any
trouble. I should say the ticket price helped quite a bit in making
this an easier decision for the organization ;). But I thought it was
a chance to motivate my team with an event of this kind. If
you have a team of developers, well, start saving up
for next year and take them to StartechConf 2012. You'll see how good
everyone feels; it's a way of optimizing through happiness. Which
brings me to the talk I liked best.

{{<figure caption="Beer!! photo shared by \@mojombo http://instagr.am/p/SyNF2/" src="cervezas.jpg">}}

Optimizing for Happiness is the name of the talk by [Tom Preston-Werner](http://tom.preston-werner.com/), cofounder of
[GitHub](https://github.com/). I'm not going to go into the details of this
talk, because I think the ideas he raises deserve a post of their own.
But if you're an entrepreneur, or an executive, or the head of a team of
any kind, not necessarily in technology development, you should
ask yourself what people's intrinsic motivators
are. And one of them is that people seek to be happy (or to feel
happy as much of the time as possible, to be
[consistent with my view of happiness](/en/blog/lnds/2010/10/02/ser-and-estar/)).

The leitmotif of
[\@mojombo](https://twitter.com/#!/mojombo)'s talk is framed as a
question: what is the ROI of happiness? What is the return on
investment in the team's happiness? If the people you work with are
happy, then they won't just be more productive, they'll produce a better
quality product or service. I won't go on about it, but I'll
leave you a link to a book Tom recommends in his talk: [Drive: The
Surprising Truth About What Motivates Us](http://amzn.to/vVwNxh) by
David Pink[^1].

Finally, I want to thank and congratulate the organizing team.
They really showed that it's possible to put on a world-class
conference in this country. Having been to several here and
abroad, I can tell you this conference is of very high quality. Congratulations; I think
you've made a great contribution to the country, and you set a very high bar for
many of the conferences held here (including the ones put on by
big companies and multinationals; you did it better, believe me).

In particular, a big shout-out to Leo Soto, one of the organizers, one of the
really good developers we have in Chile, whom I had the
honor of meeting. I'm eagerly awaiting the 2012 edition.
