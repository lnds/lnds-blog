> I'm going to Wichita\
> Far from this opera for evermore\
> I'm gonna work the straw\
> Make the sweat drip out of every pore \
> --- The White Stripes


When I was in school I was in a band that played Latin rock. We played
parties and events in Chuquicamata and Calama, the mining towns of
northern Chile. Several years later every member of the group was in
Santiago, so we got together for a few months to rehearse, in a garage
or a little storage room, I don't quite remember, at the house of our
second guitarist's father.


The idea was to end up playing in some pub in the capital. But the idea
went nowhere. We split up and for years we didn't get back together,
until August 2000 came around and I suggested we improvise something
for my wedding. It was our worst performance ever, but in the end we
got through a couple of Miguel Mateos songs, played White Stripes
style, with just a guitar and drums. That saved the night.

## CEO

The months went by, and 2001 arrived, with the internet bubble and
everyone's craze to start something on the web. Around then a
brother-in-law of mine, who advised Conicyt, Chile's national science
agency, told me they were planning to invite some "young veterans of
the internet" to a meeting to explore activities to organize together.
He asked me for some names, and I told him I was interested in taking
part too.

That's how one day in 2001 we found ourselves in the office of [Eric Goles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Goles), winner of the National
Prize for Exact Sciences, academic and at the time the director of
[Conicyt](https://www.conicyt.cl/). I remember one wall had a
beautiful large-format painting hanging on it. A modern painting that
showed the Machine and the face of Alan Turing, and of course a
half-eaten apple, all in a modern, well-executed composition. It was
signed by a well-known artist. Goles told us how he had commissioned
the piece and then started telling us the story of Turing, a somewhat
unnecessary gesture, because almost everyone in that office that day
was a former student of his, and besides, we knew Turing's story
perfectly well. But that's how he was, so you had to be patient. (Around
that time Eric Goles had a popular science show on television, so I
suppose he felt called to be the Chilean Carl Sagan, what do I know).

{{<figure caption="Image from the CEO.cl website, a historical record of those years that is worth a visit" src="https://d2dspjyoh5c79p.cloudfront.net/83619d8e-53d6-11e9-8e69-21c4c306beae-aa9f18b7">}}


So over the months this group kept up the habit of getting together
for lunch every so often, lunches to which we invited entrepreneurs,
officials, and people interested in building a climate of
entrepreneurship in Chile. We became independent of the state agency
and held these activities at the offices of the company of one of the
group's members.

Out of those lunches came [CEO: Clima de Emprendimiento
Organizado](https://www.ceo.cl/newtenberg/609/article-1271.html) (Organized Entrepreneurship Climate). Deep
down we all felt something significant would come out of these
meetings, but the great achievement was keeping those gatherings going
for several years.

Time passed, September 11, 2001 shook us all, and with that event
began, in my opinion, the era we live in, with its wars on terrorism,
fake news, distrust and lack of patience.

## Post-punk revival

> I say don't you know\
> You say you don't know\
> I say, take me out!\
> --- Franz Ferdinand


The [Post Punk Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-punk_revival),
or Garage Rock Revival, was a movement that started in the late
nineties and ran through the early 2000s. They drew on grunge, new
wave, blues and punk. An interesting mix, with bands like the White
Stripes, Franz Ferdinand, Elastica or Arctic Monkeys.

[I don't think it's a coincidence that the White Stripes chose to use
the colors red and white and to invent an origin story that tied them
to the American heartland. Or that Franz Ferdinand took the name of the
Archduke of Austria, whose assassination led to the First World War.
This musical style, with its lyrics and aesthetics, reflected the start
of our contemporary era, with its invasions of the Middle East and its
search for weapons of mass destruction that never
existed.]{style="letter-spacing: 0.01rem;"}

## November 2001

> Hate to say I told you so, alright\
> Come on\
> I do believe I told you so\
> -- The Hives

Perhaps one of the happiest moments of my life was the birth of my
daughter Francisca, in late November 2001. Holding her in my arms was a
moment of total joy that is hard to beat.

But this lovely personal story could have turned into a drama worthy of
Charles Dickens. After the birth there were unexpected complications
and Kika had to stay in the hospital for several days. They were hard
times, full of uncertainty. For a month my duty was to my wife and my
daughter, above everything else, and I was at the hospital that whole
time without going to work.

I remember that the Isapre, the private health insurer I had at the
time, sent an account executive to see me, and she told me there was
nothing we could do anymore because I hadn't requested the catastrophic
coverage in time. Can you understand that?

[At that moment, when I was worried because my wife was spending the
night in the ICU. Moments when I asked them to hand me the baby so I
could feed her and hold her while tears ran from my eyes, because I was
desperate and didn't know what might happen. Right then the system was
asking me to fill out a form, to activate a damn catastrophic
insurance policy? Can't that be automatic? Or if you request it after
the fact, once things have calmed down a bit, can't they honor it?

Those are the bugs our representatives leave in our legislation and
that end up hurting people, feeding this climate of distrust in
everything and everyone that defines our time.


## Leaving Las Vegas

> I'm standing in the middle of the desert\
> Waiting for my ship to come in\
> --- Sherryl Crow


We got home, with the baby and with my wife recovering from the ordeal.
For months she had to keep up with treatments and support. But she got
better fast. The baby was starting to grow and to receive the love of
her older siblings.

Still, I wasn't at ease. I was in debt and not very happy with my
salary either. And the mood of the time was entrepreneurship. I had
watched many friends take the leap. A colleague, a deputy manager, left
the Chamber to set up his own venture.

That's how I got an interesting offer from one of the CEO participants.
I would be in charge of building the technology area of his startup.
The offer was for almost 30% more than I was making at the time. I
talked to my boss. I still wasn't convinced I wanted to leave for good,
because it meant a risk, so I was hoping they'd make me a counteroffer.
After all, I had been quite successful and had contributed a lot in my
job.

My boss reacted and did what he was supposed to do. I felt he didn't
want to let me go, and I'll always be grateful to him for that. But it
didn't go well for him. Apparently I wasn't that valuable to the rest
of the organization. The counteroffer was ridiculous. Jorge presented
it to me with some disappointment and embarrassment, I think.

That's how I began my exit from the Santiago Chamber of Commerce. But
at the time I didn't feel sad. On the contrary, I was angry. My exit
was pretty abrupt: within a few days I was at another company preparing
the ground to launch a new startup.

## Toxic environments

> Due to lack of interest\
> Tomorrow is cancelled\
> -- Kaiser Chiefs


Sometimes a leak of toxic material starts poisoning a river, or a
lagoon. The leak may be small, but it's steady.

That happens in organizations. There are toxic leaks we don't plug in
time. This toxicity shows up between people and in their relationships.
Not everyone in a company has to like each other, but, in my opinion,
it's the duty of those who run organizations to keep these toxic leaks
from happening.

{{<figure caption="Watch out for the toxic elements that spill into the river of an organization's creativity and effort." src="https://d2dspjyoh5c79p.cloudfront.net/7470df50-53de-11e9-8e69-21c4c306beae-aa9f18b7">}}

I felt that back then. I knew some people in the organization where I
had worked until then didn't like me. It gets complicated when the
people who don't like you are people with power. I don't know whether
it was conscious or unconscious, but that was becoming unbearable for
me too.

You have to learn how to handle yourself inside organizations. To know
their dynamics, their politics. Every human organization is political.
A lot of young people don't get that. They think it's all about
technical merit, but that is often the least important thing in
building a career.

Every organization has factions, with their own interests. That's
normal, even healthy. I've always been suspicious of any group of
people where everything is cordial and nobody disagrees.

What matters, and the key to leadership in my opinion, is that despite
these differences everyone is aligned behind the company's mission and
vision. When personal interests, or those of the factions, stray from
the path that was laid out, that's when institutions begin to decay.
That's when people decide to walk away and look for other horizons.

And so in 2002 I was heading into the darkest period of my professional
life, but I didn't know it. I had the support of my wife, a beautiful
baby and my kids. Without them I don't know how I would have survived
what came next, but that's something I'll tell you about in the
[next part](/en/blog/lnds/2019/04/06/the-end-of-agile-vi/).
