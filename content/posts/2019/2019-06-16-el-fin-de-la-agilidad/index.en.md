In February 2011 we had put into production one of the most ambitious
projects my team had taken on up to that point.

We met the deadline, although technically the system wasn't finished.
As [I explained before](/en/blog/lnds/2019/05/21/the-end-of-agile-ix/),
if we commit to a date our focus is on meeting the deadline. Management
is aimed at hitting that milestone first; if that requires cutting
scope, then scope gets cut. Another important measure is risk
management: keeping everyone informed of the impediments that stand in
the way of the date and taking steps to mitigate them. So once the
go-live milestone was met, a later stage of continuous improvement
began, to stabilize the system and cover as many of the requirements in
the original scope as possible.

## Boston

> "I\'m shipping up to Boston\
> --- Dropkick Murphys

After that milestone, in May 2011 I traveled to Boston, Massachusetts,
to attend that year's Red Hat Summit. I had also received recognition
from the company for my work in those first years, so I was feeling
happy.

![](https://d2dspjyoh5c79p.cloudfront.net/b63e601a-905d-11e9-8e69-21c4c306beae-aa9f18b7)

The event was interesting, because there I got to attend [the launch of
the first version of
Openshift](https://www.lnds.net/blog/2011/05/red-hat-jboss-summit-2011.html).

That is how the memories of what I saw on [that visit to
Borland in the 90s](https://www.lnds.net/blog/lnds/2019/3/18/el-fin-de-la-agilidad)
came back to life. I wanted us to work with continuous integration. It
would still be a couple of years before we started implementing this
technology in our company.

One of the first important changes of that period also took place.
Rubén Valenzuela told us he had decided to accept an offer from Oracle
and would be leaving the company. That meant a major restructuring. So
we decided to split our systems area: the team of DBAs and systems
engineers would join Previred's main systems team. The support and QA
group would report to me. So we began the search for someone to take
charge of this area, which we temporarily called the "operations
area". That is how Elizabeth Sepúlveda joined our team.

That year Carolina Rojas also took charge of the entire development
team, and that is how the core organization of my team was born.

The **power trio** that would support my management over these years
was born at that time, although it wouldn't be consolidated until a
good while later. Carolina Rojas in charge of project management and
the team of developers, Elizabeth Sepúlveda in quality control and
support, and Miguel González in architecture. These three people were
with me in 2011, but their titles and final positions took shape over
several years.

## Power Trio

In rock, a [power trio](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_trio) [is a
lineup originally made up of an electric guitar, an electric bass and a
drum kit. This lineup was born in the sixties; some of the first
examples are Cream and The Jimi Hendrix
Experience.]{style="letter-spacing: 0.01rem;"}

ZZ Top is an example of a famous Texas blues power trio, formed in
1970. In prog we have Rush, and Emerson, Lake and Palmer; in the latter
case the guitar is replaced by synthesizers.

And one of the best power trios of the eighties is of course The
Police. When they came to Chile in 2008, my sister gave me tickets to
see them for my birthday. It was an incredible night, seeing the three
Brits and in particular Stewart Copeland, one of my favorite drummers,
live.

The three Ps
------------

> When the world is running down\
> You make the best of what\'s still around\
> --- The Police

As a leader, administrator, manager, boss, your main concern should be
three things, Julie Zhuo advises in her book: [The
Making of a
Manager.](https://www.amazon.com/Making-Manager-What-Everyone-Looks/dp/0735219567)

![](https://d2dspjyoh5c79p.cloudfront.net/215b91f8-8f9c-11e9-8e69-21c4c306beae-aa9f18b7)

I call them the 3 Ps of management:

**Purpose, People and Process**

Purpose is about defining the reason, the Why that Simon Sinek talks
about in [his famous TED
talk](https://www.ted.com/talks/simon_sinek_how_great_leaders_inspire_action?language=es).
Sinek doesn't propose a business strategy in that talk; what he
explains is the way leaders inspire. It's important to draw that
distinction.

The second element is people, that is, the who. They are the most
important element; they are the ones the vision must be addressed to.

The third element of this golden triangle is the how, that is, the
process. Take care to establish and communicate the purpose well,
choose the right people, then set up and continuously improve the
process, which is the way things get done. That is the key to start
moving forward.

This is a true power trio that, I believe, will let you pull through
on every project you take on and commit to.

***Purpose, People and Process.***

At the center of the triangle the what emerges: the product, the
result of your management.

## Coda

> Synchronicity\
> A connecting principle,\
> Linked to the invisible\
> Almost imperceptible \
> -- The Police

I just want to add one more thing.

Kierkegaard tells us that life consists of a series of decisions we
make day after day. Our existence is, in the end, the result of those
decisions. We make those decisions based on what we are and what we
want to be.

*"Life is not a problem to be solved but a reality to be
experienced".*

You'll understand, then, that in 2011 this triangle I've shown you was
not a concept I had clear or defined. I was barely moving forward,
making the best decisions that seemed right to me at the time. Every
choice was a bet. Every bet had results and consequences. Not every
decision was the right one, and we made many mistakes.

Kierkegaard says "Life can only be understood looking backward, but it
must be lived looking forward". What the Danish philosopher is telling
us is that to understand what we are we must look to our past.

This series has let me understand many things about who I am as a
person, as well as how our team came to be and to work the way it does.
But the most important thing is that we mustn't get trapped in the
past. There are situations we can no longer change, and staying stuck
lamenting them is dying slowly. I refuse to do that, of course. When I
feel calm and complete it will mean I'm ready and resigned to die. So
as valuable and entertaining as these memoirs are, the best is yet to
come.
