Alejandro Fuentes, from [CSOL](https://www.csol.org/), was kind enough
to [comment on my previous post](/en/blog/lnds/2010/09/19/state-of-open-source-in-chile-chileforge/),
and he says something I liked a lot:

> [...]On the other hand, trying to measure or extrapolate the "state of
> free software in Chile" has the same methodological value as trying
> to establish the "state of Chilean painting" by counting the pictures
> in a museum.[...]

He's right, but I think he misread my intent, or I picked the wrong
title.

Either way, the museum metaphor is a good one (it reminds me of the
Indiana Jones line: "[That belongs in a museum](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0097576/quotes)"), 
because the Forges do, in fact, end up being nothing but museums. What's needed
are living, collaborative communities. That's why I think the idea of
GobForge is a waste of time, now that we've evolved to social-network
models for developers, like [GitHub](https://www.github.com), [BitBucket](https://bitbucket.org/), and even [CodePlex](http://www.codeplex.com). That will be the subject of future posts, so don't rush me.

The point of this post was to tack a little tail onto my previous analysis, with
a visit to the software museum of the Chilean government, or of the Ministry
of Economy, to be more precise (you'll see why).

**A brief analysis of GobForge**

GobForge is part of the new [public software](http://www.softwarepublico.cl) initiative promoted by the Secretaría de
Desarrollo Digital (the government's digital development office).

The site appears to have been created in 2008.

**Registered users:** 141

**Total registered projects:** 16

**Total cataloged projects:** 11 (2 in Beta, 9 in
Production/Stable)

**Activity**

The home page has several news items announcing that
various documents or files have been made available. There are only 6
news items.

The success of open source projects depends on the activity of their
community. This isn't just a museum, it's a museum that doesn't
even have a guide you can turn to.

**Project maturity**

There are 11 cataloged projects:

-   2 in beta, which have not released a single file

-   9 in production.

-   8 of the production/stable projects have released
    files

-   8 of the production/stable projects use Subversion
    (SVN) to manage code.

-   Every project was created by the administrator user,
    Admin. The 8 SVN repositories were initialized by the same
    user (jhp).

-   Almost every project has just 1 member (Admin)

-   Every cataloged project was developed at the
    Ministry of Economy.

**Other observations**

GobForge gives the impression of a site thrown together in a hurry, as if
someone had issued a peremptory order. 8 of the production projects
were created on the morning of April 4, 2009. All the
repositories were created by the same user, and they've had no
activity since.

**Comment**

It's not enough to ask a consultant to put together a SourceForge-style site
and populate the SVN repositories so you can say you're doing a
good job and that 
[we're promoting free software in the government](http://www.paulosaavedra.cl/blog/2010/08/17/software-libre-en-el-estado-de-chile/).
All of us in this market can easily work out
who the consultant was who went to the trouble of standing this site up (someone
I think highly of). Good thing he did his job well! Otherwise the
government would have nothing to show. But the people who received this
work, what did they do next? The authorities and the people in charge
behind it, what have they done to make this community grow and support
its users? It's a nice initiative, but it looks like nothing more than a virtual
ribbon-cutting at a badly run museum.
