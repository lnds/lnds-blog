At my job there are disaster recovery plans and contingency sites, and
they get tested at least a couple of times a year,
all duly audited because there are government requirements and, stronger
still, requirements from the foreign institutions that own
the company. It's not work anyone likes doing, but when
an incident like an earthquake hits, the truth is it helps a lot, and
it lets our IT people stay pretty calm in that
moment. In the last earthquake everything went fine.

But in our personal lives we're not so strict. If our PC dies we
almost always end up mourning the lost data. Never mind our
personal blog...

This Friday I suffered my own personal disaster: the hard drive of the
server that hosts this blog failed beyond recovery.
Murphy had it in for me.

I've been put to the test by the IT gods, and here I am recovering from
my own disaster. The backups were out of date, of course.
On top of that, this wasn't the only site on that server. My son
hosts several of his projects and his clients' there, and so do my wife,
my brother and a friend. I'm ashamed to admit they have
better backups than I do, me, the one who preaches and doesn't practice.

Me and my big mouth (or my big blog, as Jens put it).

I hope to have all the articles recovered during the day. I'm
loading them month by month into another space, so they may take a while
to show up. For now comments are disabled, because
Movable Type hasn't thought through article backups very well, or I'm
too clumsy to figure out how to do them. If you want to give me advice,
you can use twitter ([\@lnds](http://twitter.com/lnds)) or write me an
email (to the user ediaz at this domain).
