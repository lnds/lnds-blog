> "In 2006 nobody in Chile had heard of facebook, and very few used the internet.
> In 2008 it exploded; in less than two years most people had joined the
> social network. The technological capacity to create a world of zombies is
> in place. Thousands of hours online can lead us to a new kind of
> zombie, the gadget zombie, the device zombie, permanently connected to
> the computer, the zombie who believes he is part of something
> important, but is really only there to feed the
> servers that data-mine his behavior, to be
> redirected to [consume new products.](http://www.cadaunadas.net/2010/10/3-anos-la-membrana-de-google.html)"
> ([a world of zombies, on akarrú](https://www.akarru.com/blog/2010/11/14/un-mundo-de-zombis/))

In "[a world of zombies]https://www.akarru.com/blog/2010/11/14/un-mundo-de-zombis/)",
I decided to explore the rise of the contemporary zombie myth. I'm not
done with that concept; there is a lot more to cover. Every topic
is a continent that deserves to be explored in depth.

In "You Are Not a Gadget", Jaron Lanier's central idea is that
Web 2.0 is making us uniform, even reducing us to a plane of
lower quality than we deserve as humans. Even the concept of
friendship gets degraded:

> "It depends on how you define yourself. An individual who is
> receiving a flow of reports about the romantic status of a group
> of friends must learn to think in terms of the flow for it to
> be perceived as something worth reading. So here is another
> example of how people are able to diminish themselves in order to make
> the computer seem accurate. Am I accusing all those
> millions of users of social networking sites of lowering
> themselves in order to use their services? Well, yes, I am." 
> -- Jaron Lanier, You Are Not a Gadget: A Manifesto, page 36.

Lanier's ideas are interesting, and they lead me to think about this
concept of a new kind of zombie, the device zombie, who follows the
patterns imposed by the user interface, or follows the models of
socialization defined in a wireframe, so that they fit the business
model pitched to a venture capitalist.

For Lanier, Wikipedia seeks to eliminate point of view, facebook
standardizes the way you define your identity, and twitter goes as far as
limiting the length of your message. We are adjusted to the model of the
social sites, to fit the needs of the algorithms
programmed in PHP, so that our behavior makes it possible to display
the ads that sustain the business model. In short, we
turn into device zombies.
