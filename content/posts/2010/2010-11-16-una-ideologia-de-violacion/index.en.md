One of the goals of this blog is to cast doubt on the ideas going
around, especially in tech circles. This text is by Jaron Lanier, and it
raises an important ethical question, particularly for those of us who
are interested in security. The text is Jaron Lanier's; the links and
notes are mine.

> **An Ideology of Violation**
>
> by Jaron Lanier
>
> All those nontechnical, ignorant, innocent people out there are going
> about their lives thinking that they are safe, when in actuality they
> are terribly vulnerable to those who are smarter than they are.
> Therefore, we smartest technical people ought to invent ways to attack
> the innocents, and publicize our results so that everyone is alerted
> to the dangers of our superior powers. After all, a clever evil person
> might come along.

That is Lanier's own summary of the ideology. The full text starts from
the voluntary code of conduct that Jimmy Wales and Tim O'Reilly proposed
after the [bullying
of](http://www.simdalom.com/blog/2007/04/04/blogs-codigo-de-conducta-caso-kathy-sierra/)
[Kathy Sierra](http://www.simdalom.com/blog/2007/04/04/blogs-codigo-de-conducta-caso-kathy-sierra/)[^1],
which went nowhere; moves on to the researchers who spent two years
working out how to use mobile phone technology to hack pacemakers and
switch them off by remote control in order to kill a person \[2\], and
to the ones who published how to pick one of the toughest locks around
and compared their triumph to Turing cracking Enigma \[3\]; defends
"security through obscurity", which in biology goes by another name,
biodiversity; and closes by noting that anyone who complains about the
rituals of the elite violators will be accused of spreading FUD(\*).

[^1]: The Kathy Sierra case still stirs up controversy and dispute in the
[Wikipedia
entry](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk:Kathy_Sierra#NPOV-tag) that
covers it.

[^2]: Yes, there are academics who devote themselves to thinking up
ingenious ways of killing people with mobile phones, and a lot of people
in the security community think doing this is
fine: [http://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2008/03/hacking*medical*1.html](http://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2008/03/hacking_medical_1.html)

[^3]: The Enigma machine was used by the Nazis to transmit encrypted
messages during World War II. Alan Turing and a team at
Bletchley Park managed to break the code this device generated,
probably shortening the war.

(\*) FUD stands for Fear, Uncertainty and Doubt.
