> The Consejo para la Transparencia (Chile's Transparency Council) has
> informed the Electoral Service (SERVEL) that, for the sake of
> transparency, it can no longer sell personal data, but must hand it
> over free of charge to anyone who asks for it.
>
> Source:
> <http://www.elmostrador.cl/opinion/2010/06/09/la-traicion-del-consejo-de-la-transparencia/>

The state of personal data handling in Chile is pretty bad, and with
this the Transparency Council has made it even worse.

Personal data cannot be passed around like this. The big problem we have
in Chile is the existence of a single identifier, the
[RUT](/en/blog/lnds/2009/05/19/your-id-number-please/) (the national ID number), which
makes it possible to identify people and cross-reference their
information. Add to that the fact that it can now be completed with
people's home addresses (a field present in the electoral roll
database). With that and a bit of business intelligence you can learn a
lot about people, far more than they want. This is a profound mistake.

This decision is a regrettable blunder (to avoid using stronger words).
