Let's recall what Dijkstra tells us about software engineering:

> As economics is known as "The Miserable Science", software
> engineering should be known as "The Doomed Discipline", doomed because
> it cannot even approach its goal since its goal is self-contradictory.
> Software engineering, of course, presents itself as another worthy
> cause, but that is eyewash: if you carefully read its literature and
> analyse what its devotees actually do, you will discover that software
> engineering has accepted as its charter "How to program if you
> cannot."
>
> (see [The doomed
> discipline](/en/blog/lnds/2010/01/10/the-doomed-discipline/))

Well, Dijkstra digs a little deeper in his famous answers to engineering
students, which were [kindly translated into Spanish by Javier
Smaldone](http://blog.smaldone.com.ar/2006/12/24/citas-de-dijkstra/). A
short excerpt of the original:

### Answers to questions from students of Software Engineering {#respuestasapreguntasdeestudiantesdeingenieradesoftware}

[The approximate reconstruction of the questions is left as an exercise
to the reader.]

-   It is not the task of the University to offer what society asks for,
    but to give what society needs.
-   The required techniques of effective reasoning are pretty formal,
    but as long as programming is done by people that don't master them,
    the software crisis will remain with us and will be considered an
    incurable disease. And you know what incurable diseases do: they
    invite the quacks and charlatans in, who in this case take the form
    of Software Engineering gurus.
-   In the software business there are many enterprises for which it is
    not clear that science can help them; that science should try is not
    clear either.

Along the way Dijkstra quotes two of his earlier notes,
[EWD898](http://www.cs.utexas.edu/users/EWD/transcriptions/EWD08xx/EWD898.html)
(1984) and [EWD920](http://www.cs.utexas.edu/users/EWD/ewd09xx/EWD920.PDF) (1985).

Austin, 28 November 2000

**prof. dr. Edsger W. Dijkstra** Department of Computer Sciences\
The University of Texas at Austin\
Austin, TX 78712 - 1188\
