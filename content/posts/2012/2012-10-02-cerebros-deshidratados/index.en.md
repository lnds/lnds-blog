In November 2011, backed by the conclusions of a group of
scientists who, in 3 years of research, could not find
conclusive evidence, the European Union banned drink
producers from claiming that *"water can prevent
dehydration"*.[^1]

Yes, just as you read it: in Europe a bottled water producer
cannot include in its advertising the claim that "water
hydrates", or prevents dehydration, to be more precise.

Let's think for a moment. *Hydro* comes from the Greek[^2]  ὑδρο-, hydros, which
means\.... wait a minute!\.... WATER!!

Well, this situation didn't last long. After a few days, and after
being ridiculed, the scientists of the European Food Safety
Authority (EFSA) issued a statement
reporting that there is indeed enough evidence to prove that
water can regulate body temperature and allow the body
to carry out its normal physical and cognitive functions[^3] (apparently
hydration produces the same effects, but you can't
say that, remember?).

## And why don't they legislate on more important things? 

In any case, instead of legislating that kind of stupidity they could
ban the proliferation and use of R-718, because I received extremely
serious information about this compound, which I now proceed to
transcribe.

R-718, or dihydrogen monoxide:

-   Is also called hydroxylic acid, and is the major component of
    acid rain.

-   Contributes to the greenhouse effect.

-   Can cause severe burns.

-   Inhaling it is fatal.

-   Contributes to soil erosion.

-   Can cause electrical failures and reduce the effectiveness of
    automobile brakes

-   Has been found in tumors removed from terminal cancer
    patients

And despite all those effects this dangerous compound is used

-   As an industrial solvent and coolant.

-   In nuclear plants.

-   In the production of polystyrene.

-   As a fire retardant.

-   In many cruel experiments on animals, especially
    marine mammals.

-   In the distribution of pesticides. Even after being washed,
    many products remain contaminated with this chemical.

-   And as an additive in junk food and other food products.

This compound is a real danger to the environment and nobody does
anything. Not even in Europe, with its high levels of concern for the
environment: they bother to legislate so that no ad says
that water hydrates, and they let this use and abuse of dihydrogen
monoxide slide.[^4] Inconceivable.
