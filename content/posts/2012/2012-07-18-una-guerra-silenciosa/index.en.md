I'm a GitHub fan ([as you can
see for yourselves](/en/blog/lnds/2012/02/22/the-lord-of-the-files/)),
but I also really like what they do at Atlassian. For years
I have used
[Jira](http://www.atlassian.com/es/software/jira/overview) and pushed my colleagues to use it. I use
both companies' tools personally and at work.

Personally I keep some public and private repositories on
[Github](http://www.github.com/), and I never considered using
[Bitbucket](https://bitbucket.org/), Atlassian's option. Basically
because to me Bitbucket was a Mercurial-based repository, and I'm not
very fond of that DVCS. As Scott Chacon says, [Mercurial feels like
a Git Lite](http://schacon.github.com/2008/11/24/on-mercurial.html).

But it turns out that since late last year BitBucket supports Git, and
it has an aggressive campaign that lets you create an account with unlimited
public and private repositories plus 5 users for zero dollars (zero,
nothing, free).

That is a tempting offer, considering as well that BitBucket is
practically a [GitHub clone](http://www.pocoo.org/~blackbird/github-vs-bitbucket/bitbucket.html)
(with all the good, the bad and the ugly that implies).

Atlassian is an Australian company that has had several years of good
results. Its first big injection of money came in 2010, when
it received 60 million dollars of VC money, and last year it announced its
first big investment, in cloud9, a SaaS IDE. Before that
it had acquired BitBucket. They have clearly expanded, and the product
portfolio is very well integrated.

In May of this year they released Stash, a product for managing
Git repositories in enterprise environments (or on your own
servers). It's an elegant solution, very well integrated with Jira. I've
been looking into it these past few days and I like it quite a bit.

But Stash competes directly with GitHub Enterprise,
GitHub's product aimed at the enterprise market. GitHub Enterprise is
licensed by number of users, starting at a cost of 5,000
dollars for 20 users. Stash starts, in its entry-level version, at 10
dollars, and from there jumps to 1,800 dollars for a base of 18 to 25
users. Of course Stash has fewer features than GitHub,
but that may well change; they will probably bring in
BitBucket features to make this product even more
competitive.

On the other hand, GitHub has just received its first round of
funding, through the VC firm of Mark Andreseen (the creator of Mosaic,
Netscape, Ning, etc.). The VC firm [Andreseen-Horowitz](http://a16z.com/) has
invested 100 million dollars in GitHub!

[\@Mojombo](https://twitter.com/mojombo/) (Tom Preston-Werner)
explained it on his [blog](https://github.com/blog/1189-investing-in-github):

> We loved it when Marc Andreessen proclaimed, "[Software is eating the
> world](http://online.wsj.com/article/SB10001424053111903480904576512250915629460.html)."

Marc\'s venture firm, [Andreessen Horowitz](http://a16z.com/), is
younger than GitHub. And like GitHub, they\'re trying to do things
differently. They believe in software as the future of everything. They
want to
help [founders](http://bhorowitz.com/2010/04/28/why-we-prefer-founding-ceos/) build
great companies. They clearly have no interest in the status quo of
venture capital.

Over the past few months we\'ve gotten to know Marc and one of his
partners, Peter Levine, and we really like them. We wish we could hire
them both, but they already have jobs. So instead we\'re going to work
with them through their firm.

So that's where things stand. An inevitable clash is coming. I suspect
Atlassian and Github will start running into each other more and more, and
Atlassian's aggressive pricing campaign is an attempt to counter the goodwill
GitHub generates, especially among the more hardcore developers.

This will probably be good for us developers,
but it also creates several dilemmas. After all, the Atlassian suite
is localized (translated) into Spanish (something it would be nice to see in
GitHub's products), the Atlassian product suite is very good, and in
enterprise environments they are well regarded and increasingly used. BitBucket's
price is a major deciding factor, especially in our Latin American
reality: having a cloud platform to maintain and operate
our private source code repositories at zero cost is very
attractive.

What's going to happen? I don't know. A war may break out, and I hope
we developers don't get hit too hard. After all, these are two
companies that have built very good tools for everyone. Let's hope
money doesn't end up ruining it all. What do you think?
