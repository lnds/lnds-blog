Although the cotton picker was invented around 1850, it wasn't until
1943 that it became useful enough to be put to work on farms.

![](800px-Baumwoll-Erntemaschine_auf_Feld.jpeg)

Only in the early 1950s did the machine start to be introduced in the
southern United States, and it quickly destroyed the market for the one
thing vast numbers of Black workers in the South had to offer: their
labor.

All this set off a migration of thousands of African Americans to the
more industrialized cities of the North, such as Detroit, Chicago and
New York, but also to other centers in the South that needed labor for
their factories, such as Atlanta and Birmingham. This is the moment when
the real emancipation of Black Americans, and their struggle for their
rights, begins.

The introduction of this piece of technology, the cotton harvesting
machine, probably explains the civil rights movement of the early 1960s.

Does technology shape man?
