At the parents' meeting for my youngest daughter's class, the teacher told us about the
values model they are working on with the children at the school. She talked about
the 3 Rs: Responsibility, Repair and Reconciliation. The idea is that
when a child makes a mistake, they must take responsibility, step
forward and admit that they were wrong, or that they did something they shouldn't have,
such as picking on a classmate. The second thing is that they must
repair the harm done. And if it affects another person, as in a fight,
they must reconcile, with one apologizing and the other sincerely accepting
the apology.

Regardless of whether we fully agree with that model, what
struck me as remarkable is that the teacher said one of the problems of
our country is that nobody wants to take responsibility for their actions, and that
this shows above all in our political authorities, who dodge
responsibility. I found that very much on the mark.

I think it is very important for us to understand that we are responsible for
our actions, and that if we make mistakes we must repair them, and not
make excuses, or dodge them, or ignore them. Something that is so common in our
society. And if we don't ask forgiveness for our mistakes, if we don't take
responsibility, if we don't repair, how do we expect reconciliation?

And I'm not only talking about politics; at work too, and in our lives,
it's time we did things well and took
responsibility for our actions.
