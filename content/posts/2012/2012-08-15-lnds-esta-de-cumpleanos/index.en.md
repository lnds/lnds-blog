# It's LNDS's birthday 

By Alejandro Barros

[www.alejandrobarros.com](http://www.alejandrobarros.com)

A few days ago I received a welcome invitation to write a few lines for the
seventh anniversary of Eduardo Díaz's blog -- La Naturaleza del
Software ([\@lnds](https://twitter.com/lnds)), an invitation I am grateful for
and honored by.

I want to start by congratulating Eduardo on his perseverance in this business of
writing and keeping up a blog, which is proven by how long he has been
at it and by the more than 1,000 posts published. I say this from up close, since
in my case it is something I enjoy a lot and to which I have also devoted
many hours. I wonder: has Eduardo, with his permanent quest for
metrics and algorithms, calculated the number of man-hours spent
producing content (finding information, analyzing it, writing and reviewing
the material before publishing) for his blog? And that is without counting the time
that goes into building templates and improving the site. I leave
that task to you, since I estimated it a while back and you could build several
complex programs with it.

Going through Eduardo's blog, what surprises you is his capacity to produce new
entries (he even has days with two!), starting with very
short posts like the one that opened his blog, "[You can't protect
software](/en/blog/lnds/2005/08/01/you-cant-protect-software/)",
 back on August 1, 2005. Let me tell you, Eduardo, we started almost
simultaneously; mine dates from June 23 of the same year. And as if
one blog weren't enough, Eduardo has two more,
[Akarru](http://www.akarru.org/blog/) and [La sombra de
Dijkstra](http://www.programando.org/blog/). How do you like that!

Eduardo and I have several things in common: engineering school, the world of
technology, but above all a taste for ICTs ;-) and for
taking on highly complex subjects in clear and easy language,
probably keeping in mind that a good percentage of the Chilean population
doesn't understand what it reads.

Eduardo and I have a healthy and constructive argument going about
ICTs, or should I say information technologies, given that I am
a guest in his virtual space and, as the saying goes, when in Rome
do as the Romans do. Another of the arguments we have had is
more related to Eduardo's optimism about the results of
technology projects, which, starting from a post of mine,
"[How IT projects behave: they owe us!](http://www.alejandrobarros.com/content/view/691759/Comportamiento-de-proyectos-TI-Estan-en-deuda.html#content-top)
", produced a reply from Eduardo with heavy artillery, [What debt are you
talking about?](/en/blog/lnds/2010/01/07/what-debt-are-you-talking-about/).
When I read it I was tempted to write a comment along the lines of "touched
a nerve, touched a nerve...", but I preferred to let Eduardo carry on with
his mistake!

On another occasion, without agreeing on it beforehand, we ran a
relay to analyze the lack of seriousness and the improvisation of our
authorities in changing the daylight saving date, Eduardo with his well
documented post from late March 2011, "[Time as a
parameter](/en/blog/lnds/2011/03/30/time-as-a-parameter/)",
which included an unfortunate tweet from the Minister of Public Works on
the matter. I had addressed the subject in the post "[The time change
requires a deeper
evaluation](http://www.alejandrobarros.com/content/view/1432514/Cambio-de-hora-requiere-evaluacion-mas-profunda.html)"
from the same period. Well, Eduardo took that seriously and did the
evaluation, showing fairly compelling evidence.

In all this time I have read many of his posts, but I clearly
go with his tribute to Alan Turing, "[The father of
computing](/en/blog/lnds/2012/06/17/the-father-of-computing/)",
which I find remarkable.

Having read some of \@lnds's posts, I will venture
to recommend him a couple of books. First, in connection with his
entry "[Atheist summer
camp](/en/blog/lnds/2009/07/01/atheist-summer-camp/)"
 from July 2009 and his taste for algorithms, I will venture to
suggest a book for him to analyze and then post about: [The
Meme Machine, by Susan
Blackmore](http://www.amazon.com/maquina-Memes-Meme-Machine-Spanish/dp/8449309670/ref=sr_1_1?s=books&ie=UTF8&qid=1344869158&sr=1-1&keywords=la+maquina+de+los+memes). Or,
to keep up his fondness for cryptography, I think a good
choice is "[The Code Book](http://www.amazon.com/Los-c%C3%B3digos-secretos-Simon-Singh/dp/848306278X)"
by Simon Singh, a very entertaining and pleasant book on the evolution of
cryptography from the dawn of humanity, when messages were
tattooed on the scalp and the hair was left to grow back (there was more time
for sending messages), up to today with quantum cryptography.

Many congratulations, and onward with your blogomania (or whatever
this disease we suffer from is called)
