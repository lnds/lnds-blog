> It was a day of pouring rain, and Marcos gets a flat tire right
> in front of an insane asylum. He pulls over, gets out, shelters from
> the rain as best he can and starts removing the lug nuts that hold the
> wheel. He leaves them carefully on the ground in a little pile. He takes
> the wheel to the trunk of the car to swap it for the spare,
> and when he comes back he accidentally kicks the lug nuts, with such bad luck that
> they roll away and drop down the storm drain.

> "What rotten luck!", Marcos exclaims, "now what am I going to
do?"

> At that moment, one of the madmen who was watching the scene from the window
calls out to him: "Hey, buddy! Easy! Take one lug nut off each of
the other wheels, put those three on this wheel, and drive like that to
the next gas station, where you can buy lug nuts and fix the
wheel."

> Marcos is astonished, and after hearing the madman's ingenious proposal
he can't help but thank him enthusiastically: "What a great idea, you nut! How
did you come up with it?"

> The madman's answer was immediate: "Because I'm locked up in here
for being crazy, not for being stupid!"

I heard this story a long time ago from my boss, and it is remarkable because
it explains an important management principle. The question, dear
readers, is: which management principle is on display in
this joke?
