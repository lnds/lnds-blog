***Fending for yourself***
======================

by [Enzo Abbagliati](http://www.cadaunadas.net/)

{{<figure src="bibquemchi.jpg" caption="Quemchi Library in Chiloé (photo taken from its Facebook page https://www.facebook.com/biblioteca.quemchi">}}

On the night of Thursday, August 30, the public library of Quemchi
(Chiloé) was robbed. According to the [information published in the
press](http://www.soychile.cl/Chiloe/Policial/2012/09/01/116292/Antisociales-desvalijaron-biblioteca-publica-de-Quemchi.aspx),
the thieves' loot was the computer equipment of the BiblioRedes
program. Just one more robbery like so many others, someone might think who doesn't know
the role that equipment plays in a public library. One more
robbery, someone will conclude who doesn't actually know what a
public library is for.

The [new human development report on
Chile](http://www.desarrollohumano.cl/informe-2012/solo_sinopsis.pdf)
prepared by the UNDP concludes that we Chileans are satisfied with
our personal lives but express high levels of
dissatisfaction with the society we live in. It would seem
contradictory. Is it possible to say that we are happy with our
personal well-being when we are critical of the society we
live in? Let's rephrase the question: can we, as individuals, show
high levels of happiness when we don't like our surroundings?

Wenceslao Unanue is already
[breaking down](http://www.elmostrador.cl/opinion/2012/08/31/una-nueva-cara-de-la-desigualdad-en-chile-pobreza-e-infelicidad/)
the UNDP data, putting the emphasis on the inversely proportional relationship
between happiness and socioeconomic level. Nothing new, you
will say. But the question Unanue reflects on is about
our perception of loneliness. While in the ABC1 group, the wealthiest, 14% of
people stated "I frequently feel alone", in group E, the
poorest, the figure reached 42%. There are no social networks, the surroundings don't
act as a support, nor does the person feel that from there they can
project themselves and build a life. And yet, on a
scale of 1 to 10, that same group gives an average of 6.7 to its
"life satisfaction". In other words, I can be happy despite
perceiving and criticizing the breakdown of my social fabric.

A first reading of the UNDP report seems to point to the triumph of the
logic of "fend for yourself", against the grain of the unrest that
since late 2010 has been showing up in the countless
demonstrations that have taken over our public life. Seen that way,
the robbery of the Quemchi library makes sense.

According to studies carried out by BiblioRedes a few years ago,
approximately 2/3 of the people served by the program (and by
extension by public libraries) live around the poverty
line or below it. They are people, citizens of our country,
for whom the library is in many cases the only option
for access to information, culture and recreation. Robbing a public
library is robbing the community, but above all robbing those who have the
least in that community. And yet robberies at libraries, while
not massive, have been a constant ever since BiblioRedes equipped them with
high-end computers in late 2002.

I don't know who is behind the Quemchi robbery or what their motives
were, but clearly in their case satisfying their needs by
"fending for themselves" meant attacking the common
good, the collective heritage of the land where
Francisco Coloane was born. It is, if you'll allow me the hyperbole, a sublime
representation of a model of society, of country, that has been
deliberately implanted in our country since the mid-1970s,
one where personal entrepreneurship is the basis of
wealth, where the freedom of the individual stands above the well-being of the
collective. A deconstruction of the commons, carefully designed and
put into practice under the only authentic revolution to take place in
our country, the capitalist one, [analyzed in detail](http://filosofiahumanidades.uahurtado.cl/ediciones/la-revolucion-capitalista-en-chile-1973-2003-2/)
by my good friend the historian Manuel Gárate Chateau.

The UNDP report does not yield contradictory results, but ones
deeply consistent with the Chile that has been under construction
since 1973. We are happy in our personal sphere, but we
dislike our collective dimension. We distrust
institutions, just as we distrust our neighbors, or feel
alienated from our graffiti-covered neighborhoods or from public transport in the
capital, where we massively choose not to pay. Because we are not
interested in what is common, what we share. Meeting others gets
in our way, makes us uncomfortable, because it forces us --like every exercise in
collective construction- to give up part of our personal autonomy, to
trade away part of our individual project, the one the UNDP
concludes we are satisfied with. The effort makes no sense, because
it possibly won't increase our happiness, in the same way that
it would make no sense for the community of Quemchi to voice its
repudiation of what happened to its library. I confess, though, that I harbor
the unfounded hope that this last thing may happen.

Software and I admire it the way one admires endangered species:

contributing to the rebuilding of the collective.
