> "Against stupidity the gods themselves contend in vain"
> -- Friedrich von Schiller


How often do we run into stupid acts in our daily lives?
It happened to me with a package that was supposed to arrive by mail on
November 4. The parcel, which came from abroad, had been in the country
for two days, and it was impossible to speed up the delivery.
Finally, on the afternoon of the 13th, the package arrived at our house.
But the most ridiculous part was that on November 25 I got an email from
Correos de Chile, the national postal service, informing me that my package had been delivered on the 14th!

I don't know, and I don't want to try to understand, how logistics and
systems work at that company, but the observed external behavior can
be classified as simply stupid.

According to the Italian economist [Carlo Maria
Cipolla](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Maria_Cipolla), a person
is stupid if they cause harm to other people, or to a group of people, without
getting any personal gain out of it, or even worse, possibly harming
themselves in the process[^1].

You see, in the world of social media, where each of us can
end up with thousands of followers, the behavior of companies, and
our own, is being monitored, for better or worse.
We have built a great social panopticon, one that wasn't even in
Foucault's worst nightmares.

But on the other hand, these same networks have become a means of
spreading human stupidity.

Stupidity is not exclusive to people; it can become
even more evident in institutions.

I learned of Cipolla's existence through this post by Benjamí
Villoslada, titled
["Cipolla y el embarque por filas en el avión"](https://medium.com/@benjami/cipolla-y-el-embarque-por-filas-en-el-avi%C3%B3n-f5c1ca14dad6) (Cipolla and boarding the plane by rows),
where he applies some of Cipolla's conclusions to conclude that the
behavior of airlines is essentially stupid, in this
respect.

In a short work titled "Allegro ma non troppo", Cipolla lays out the
"Basic Laws of Human Stupidity".

His short essay starts like this:

> "Humanity is in a deplorable state." In fact it has always
> been in that state, which is the result of the way
> life has been organized since its beginnings. All species of
> living beings have to bear their daily dose of
> "tribulations, fears, frustrations, pains and adversities." But
> human beings must also bear the tribulations caused by a group
> of people whose "nature, character and behavior" is the
> subject dealt with here.

For Cipolla the first basic law of stupidity is the following:

> *Always and inevitably everyone underestimates the number of
> stupid individuals in circulation.*
>

However high our estimate of their number, we always fall
short, because we keep discovering that people we had considered
reasonable later turn out to be stupid. And besides, day after day
we come across new stupid people who get in the way of our
activities.


Cipolla's second basic law is:

> *The probability that a certain person be stupid is
> independent of any other characteristic of that
> person.*

Nowadays we have an egalitarian view of society,
*"it is a widespread opinion that I personally (Carlo M Cipolla) do not
share"*.

What Cipolla means by this is that there are stupid people
and others who are not, but this behavior is determined by
natural factors, not cultural ones. For Cipolla the proportion of
stupid people is the same, always and everywhere, without exceptions (of
culture, era, development, etc).

To understand the third basic law, Cipolla introduces a diagram
like the following one:

{{<figure caption="Division of people according to the losses and gains in their interactions" src="https://d2dspjyoh5c79p.cloudfront.net/f36ffba2-d2b7-11e7-a030-2b5831f8ecb5-aa9f18b7">}}

In this chart we see the interaction between a person who acts and
another person, a group or society as a whole, who is on the
receiving end of the action.

On the horizontal axis we measure the gain this person gets from their
action. The vertical axis shows the gain of whoever receives the
action. Gains can be negative, zero or positive. If they are
negative we call them losses, if they are positive they are benefits.

Cipolla's third basic law says:

> *A stupid person is a person who causes losses to another person or
> to a group of persons while himself deriving no gain and
> even possibly incurring losses.*

What Cipolla does is classify people into four categories:
the helpless, who are in the upper left quadrant, the
intelligent, who sit in the upper right quadrant, the
bandits, in the lower right quadrant, and the stupid in the
lower left
quadrant.

If a person takes an action and suffers a loss, but another person
or group of people benefits, we will say that person is
helpless.

On the other hand, if this person benefits and also benefits
the others, then they are intelligent.

And if the action only benefits whoever carries it out, and
harms the others, then that person is a
bandit.

We human beings do not act consistently. Sometimes a
person may act intelligently and other times helplessly.
Only stupid people are totally consistent in every
field of action.

The perfect bandit is the one who, when he acts, obtains gains
equal to the losses he causes the other. The simplest example
is the thief. If he robs you of CLP 10,000, he gains CLP 10,000 and you lose
CLP 10,000. If we plot them, bandits will appear on the 45-degree
line that divides the lower right quadrant. However, most
bandits will not be on this line. The quadrant is split in
two: those above the line (Mi), that is, those who obtain
gains greater than the losses they cause, and those below
the line (Me), those who obtain smaller gains than the harm
they cause.

{{<figure caption="The line that divides the bandits" src="https://d2dspjyoh5c79p.cloudfront.net/5976aff3-d2ba-11e7-a030-2b5831f8ecb5-aa9f18b7">}}

Those located in the Mi region are dishonest, but intelligent. Most
bandits fall in the Me zone.

These divisions work for bandits, the helpless and the intelligent. But
the distribution of the stupid is completely different: the
stupid are concentrated along the vertical axis and below
the origin point O, the center of our diagram. The reason is that
most stupid people are fundamentally and unwaveringly stupid. They not
only cause harm to others, but also to themselves. These last ones are
the super-stupid, who will sit to the left of the vertical axis.

Like everyone else, stupid people influence other people.
Some stupid people cause only limited harm, but others can cause
enormous damage, not just to individuals, but to entire societies.

How is it possible for stupid people to reach positions of
authority?

According to Cipolla:

> "classes and castes (both lay and ecclesiastical)
> allowed a steady flow of stupid people into
> positions of power in pre-industrial societies. That role is
> filled today by political parties, bureaucracy and democracy. General
> elections are a most effective instrument for ensuring
> the stable maintenance of the fraction \[of stupid people\] among the
> powerful: they can harm everyone else without obtaining any
> gain in return for their action."

Which brings us to the fourth basic law of stupidity:

> *Non-stupid people always underestimate the damaging power of
> stupid individuals. In particular non-stupid people constantly
> forget that at all times and places and under
> any circumstances to deal and/or associate with stupid
> people always turns out to be a costly mistake.*
>

The surprising thing is that we fail to notice the destructive and
devastating power of stupidity. Complacency and contempt, or the
temptation to associate with the stupid person to use them for our own
benefit, are telling signs of this fourth law. You only have to look at many
of the political processes of recent times to notice this phenomenon
even more.

All of this leads us to the last basic law of human
stupidity:

> *A stupid person is the most dangerous type of person.\
> Corollary: A stupid person is more dangerous
> than a bandit.*

Despite the existence of stupid people, we move forward as a society. The
question is, what is it that leads us into decline?

The reason we move forward is that the distribution of people in three of
the four quadrants is larger, fortunately. However, decline
begins when:

- The stupid members of society become more active thanks to the
permissiveness of the other members

- There is a change in the composition of the non-stupid
population, with an increase in stupid bandits and stupid
helpless people.

Every country on the rise has an unavoidable number of stupid
people. But it has an unusually high percentage of intelligent
individuals who keep the stupid fraction in check and produce
enough gains for themselves and for society to make progress
a fact. In decline, the fraction of stupid people is the same, but
the stupid bandits and the stupid helpless increase, which reinforces
the power of the stupid and leads the country to ruin.
